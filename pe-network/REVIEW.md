# Political economy, c. 1962–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 307 work nodes, 16 theme nodes and 317 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[north1990]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 99 works already in the comparative politics, IPE, American politics and technology maps are reused. Political economy overlaps heavily with those fields, and many of these came from your `papers.json`, for example Olson, Iversen & Soskice, Moene & Wallerstein, Acemoglu & Robinson and Ross.
- **Top-cited articles:** 120 come from the 400 most-cited 2006–2026 articles in economics and political-economy journals, filtered to political topics. The journals are the *QJE*, *JPE*, *AER*, *Journal of Public Economics*, *Annual Review of Economics*, *Public Choice*, *European Journal of Political Economy*, *Economics & Politics*, *QJPS*, *JLEO*, *Socio-Economic Review* and *New Political Economy*. Political science journals were added on top.
- **Anchors:** 88 books and classic articles, from Buchanan & Tullock and Olson to North, Persson & Tabellini, Acemoglu & Robinson, Piketty and Baccaro & Pontusson.

One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries, not re-read this session.

## 1. Institutions, property rights and long-run development
**Claimed.** Institutions drive long-run performance `[north1990]`, and credible commitment enabled growth `[northweingast1989]`. **Settled.** Colonial-origins evidence `[ajr2001]` and its successors `[rodrik2004]`, `[ajr2005]` made institutions central. **Contested.** Human capital first `[glaeser2004]`. Property rights can harm development `[holland2023]`. **Gaps.** Mechanisms of institutional change `[mahoneythelen2010]`.

## 2. History, culture and persistence
**Settled.** Historical shocks persist `[nunn2008]`, `[dell2010]`, `[nunnwantchekon2011]`, and culture matters `[guiso2006]`, `[tabellini2010]`. **Contested.** Culture versus institutions `[rothsteinstolle2008]` and national institutions within groups `[michalopoulos2013]`. **Gaps.** Persistence findings face spatial-noise critiques.

## 3. State capacity, taxation and fiscal contracts
**Claimed.** War made states `[tilly1990]`, and rulers maximize revenue `[levi1988]`. **Settled.** Common-interest threats drive fiscal capacity `[besleypersson2009]`. **Contested.** Measurement of state capacity `[hansonsigman2021]`. **Gaps.** Digital and data capacity of states.

## 4. Inequality and the politics of redistribution
**Claimed.** The median voter demands redistribution as inequality rises `[meltzerrichard1981]`. **Settled.** That prediction often fails `[kenworthymccall2008]`, `[lupupontusson2011]`. Beliefs about fairness and mobility matter `[alesinaangeletos2005]`, `[alesina2018]`. **Contested.** Unequal responsiveness `[bartels2008]`, `[gilens2012]`. Misperceptions `[cruces2013]`, `[gimpelsontreisman2018]`. **Gaps.** The politics of wealth taxes after Piketty `[piketty2014]`.

## 5. Welfare states and social policy
**Settled.** Three worlds `[espingandersen1990]`. Retrenchment politics differ from expansion `[pierson1996]`. **Contested.** Power resources `[korpi2006]` versus employer-centered accounts `[swenson2002]`, `[mares2003]`. Insider-outsider divides `[rueda2005]`. **Gaps.** Welfare responses to automation and AI.

## 6. Varieties of capitalism and growth models
**Settled.** Coordinated and liberal market economies `[hallsoskice2001]`. **Contested.** Liberalization `[thelen2014]` and growth models `[baccaropontusson2016]` against the VoC equilibrium view. **Gaps.** China and state capitalism `[mcnally2012]`.

## 7. Central banks, monetary and fiscal institutions
**Settled.** Time inconsistency `[kydlandprescott1977]` and conservative delegation `[rogoff1985]`. Central bank independence lowers inflation `[alesinasummers1993]`. **Contested.** The democratic politics of central banks `[bernhard2002]`, `[braun2018]`. **Gaps.** Central banks and distribution after quantitative easing.

## 8. Political business cycles and economic voting
**Settled.** Opportunistic cycles `[nordhaus1975]` concentrate in new democracies `[brenderdrazen2005]`. Economic voting depends on clarity of responsibility `[duchstevenson2008]`. **Contested.** Rational versus myopic voters `[rogoff1990]`, `[achenbartels2016]`.

## 9. Interest groups, lobbying and rent seeking
**Settled.** Concentrated interests organize `[olson1965]` and buy influence `[grossmanhelpman1994]`. Connections are valuable `[fisman2001]`, `[faccio2006]`. **Contested.** Lobbying as purchase versus subsidy `[halldeardorff2006]`. **Gaps.** Firm-level lobbying data `[kim2017]`.

## 10. Political agency, accountability and selection
**Settled.** Elections discipline incumbents `[barro1973]`, `[ferejohn1986]`, and information improves accountability `[besleyburgess2002]`, `[ferrazfinan2008]`. **Contested.** Selection versus sanctioning `[ashworth2012]`.

## 11. Corruption and governance
**Settled.** Corruption hurts growth `[mauro1995]`. Audits work `[olken2007]`. **Contested.** Perceptions versus reality `[olken2009]`. Greasing the wheels `[dreher2011]`.

## 12. Finance, crises and financialization
**Settled.** Crises recur `[reinhartrogoff2009]` and bring political extremism `[funke2016]`. **Contested.** Inequality as a cause of credit booms `[rajan2010]`. **Gaps.** Financialization's political consequences `[vanderzwan2014]`.

## 13. Labor, unions and labor markets
**Settled.** Unions compress wages `[farber2021]`. Automation polarizes jobs `[autor2015]`. **Gaps.** Platform labor and organizing.

## 14. Development, public goods and distributive politics
**Settled.** Ethnic divisions lower public goods `[alesina1999]`, `[habyarimana2007]`. **Contested.** Clientelism's forms `[stokes2005]`, `[nichter2008]`. Democracy reduces favoritism `[burgess2015]`.

## 15. Regimes and economic performance
**Contested.** Democracy and growth `[przeworski2000]` versus `[acemoglu2019]`. Authoritarian institutions `[gandhiprzeworski2007]`. Fast-growing autocracies `[luoprzeworski2019]`.

## 16. Media, information and political economy
**Settled.** Media affects voting and accountability `[dellavignakaplan2007]`, `[snyderstromberg2010]`. **Contested.** Media capture `[besleyprat2006]`. **Gaps.** Autocratic information control `[hollyer2018]`, `[rozenasstukal2019]`.
