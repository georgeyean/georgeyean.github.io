# Latin American politics, c. 1950–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 357 work nodes, 19 theme nodes and 423 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[odonnell1973]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 82 works already in other maps are reused, mostly from comparative politics (democratization, authoritarian institutions, clientelism, state capacity, populism, courts). Others come from political economy (development, corruption audits, welfare), IR (human rights trials, advocacy networks), IPE and the China map (Chinese finance and trade in the region), environmental politics (the resource curse) and REP (race in Brazil).
- **Top-cited articles:** 147 come from the most-cited 2006–2026 articles in *Latin American Politics and Society*, *Latin American Research Review*, the *Journal of Latin American Studies* and the *Journal of Politics in Latin America*. Latin America articles in *Comparative Politics*, CPS, *World Politics*, APSR, AJPS, JOP, BJPS, JCR, JPR, ISQ, IO, POP, QJPS, SCID, SER, ARPS and the *Journal of Democracy* were added too.
- **Anchors:** 87 books and classic articles, from Prebisch, Hirschman, Cardoso & Faletto and O'Donnell to Linz, Collier & Collier, Mainwaring & Scully, Stokes, Yashar, Gibson, Kurtz, Holland and Lessing.

Relations are judgments of how each work's argument relates to the other's, and every relation runs from the later work to the earlier one. One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries.

## 1. Democratization and transitions
**Claimed.** Modernization brings democracy `[lipset1959]`; breakdowns turn on leaders' choices `[linzstepan1978]`; transitions are elite bargains made under uncertainty `[odonnell1986]`, and pacts shape the democracies that follow `[karl1990]`. **Settled.** Class power and labor matter `[rueschemeyer1992]`; development sustains democracies more than it creates them `[przeworski1997]`, `[boixstokes2003]`; regional diffusion is real `[brinkscoppedge2006]`. **Contested.** Distributive theories `[acemoglurobinson2006]` against the Latin American record `[kaufman2009]`, `[haggardkaufman2012]`, `[ansellsamuels2010]`; actors' normative commitments `[mainwaringperezlinan2013]` against structure. **Gaps.** State capacity as a precondition for redistributive threats `[soifer2012scid]`; landed elites and democracy `[albertus2017]`; opposition parties in early democratization `[madrid2019]`.

## 2. Bureaucratic authoritarianism, military and one-party rule
**Claimed.** The exhaustion of easy import substitution produced bureaucratic authoritarianism `[odonnell1973]`. **Settled.** The economic determinism did not hold `[collier1979]`; regime type shapes breakdown `[geddes1999]`, `[gwf2014]`. **Contested.** Why Mexico's PRI lasted: voter support bought with spending `[magaloni2006]` or resource asymmetries `[greene2007]`, `[greene2009]`. **Gaps.** How military regimes' internal organization left legacies `[falleti2010]`, and protest under electoral autocracy `[trejo2014]`.

## 3. Hybrid regimes and democratic erosion
**Claimed.** Delegative presidents weaken checks `[odonnell1994]`; hybrid regimes are distinct `[collierlevitsky1997]`, `[levitskyway2010]`. **Settled.** Erosion now comes through elected executives, not coups `[bermeo2016]`, `[levitskyziblatt2018]`, with Venezuela the regional paradigm `[mainwaring2012]`, `[corrales2015]`. **Contested.** Whether populism or weak institutions drive erosion `[weyland2013]`, `[weyland2020]`; why voters tolerate it `[svolik2019]`, `[albertusgrossman2021]`. **Gaps.** Right-wing cases such as Bolsonaro `[hunterpower2019]` and Andean variants `[cameron2018]`.

## 4. Presidentialism and executive-legislative relations
**Claimed.** Presidentialism endangers democracy `[linz1990]`, especially with multipartism `[mainwaring1993]`. **Settled.** Presidential powers vary `[shugartcarey1992]`, `[mainwaringshugart1997]`; coalitions make presidentialism work `[figueiredolimongi2000]`, `[cheibub2004]`, `[pereiramelo2012]`. **Contested.** Whether presidentialism or military legacies explain breakdown `[cheibub2007]`. **Gaps.** Presidential falls without regime breakdown `[hochstetler2006]`, `[perezlinan2007]`, `[marsteintredet2008]`, `[perezlinan2014]`.

## 5. Parties and party-system institutionalization
**Claimed.** Labor incorporation set party trajectories `[colliercollier1991]`; party systems differ in institutionalization `[mainwaringscully1995]`. **Settled.** Several systems collapsed `[mainwaring2006]`, `[dietzmyers2007]`; market reform realigned or dealigned them `[roberts2012]`, `[roberts2014]`. **Contested.** Stability versus rootedness `[lunaaltman2011]`; brand dilution `[lupu2014]`, `[lupu2016]` versus performance. **Gaps.** Programmatic structuration `[kitschelt2010]`, segmented linkages `[luna2010]` and decay since 2010 `[mainwaring2018]`.

## 6. Populism and the left turn
**Claimed.** Populism is bad macroeconomics `[dornbuschedwards1991]`. **Settled.** Populism is political, compatible with neoliberalism `[roberts1995]`, `[weyland2001]`. **Contested.** Strategy versus ideas `[weyland2001]`, `[mudde2004]`, `[hawkins2009]`, `[hawkinsrovira2017]`; why the left won: party strength `[cleary2006]`, economic voting `[murillo2010]`, issue voting `[bakergreene2011]` or inequality `[debshelmke2010]`; two lefts and rents `[weyland2009]`, `[levitskyroberts2011]`, `[mazzuca2013]`. **Gaps.** Movement-based parties `[anria2013]` and the left's legacy for informal workers `[feierherd2022]`.

## 7. Clientelism and vote buying
**Claimed.** Machines monitor voters and hold them accountable `[stokes2005]`. **Settled.** Brokers have their own agendas `[stokes2013]`; machines buy turnout `[nichter2008]`, `[gansmorse2014]`; vote buying is underreported `[gonzalezocantos2011]`. **Contested.** Top-down versus demand-driven clientelism `[nichterperess2016]`, `[nichter2018]`; brokers as problem-solvers `[auyero2000]`, `[zarazaga2014]`. **Gaps.** Why politicians opt out `[weitzshapiro2012]` and non-party brokers `[hollandpalmerrubin2015]`.

## 8. Voters, partisanship and public opinion
**Settled.** Social policy reshaped Lula's coalition `[hunterpower2007]`, `[zucco2008]`; cash transfers reward incumbents `[delao2012]`, `[zucco2013]`. **Contested.** Partisanship's roots `[samuels2006]`, `[lupu2012]`, `[samuelszucco2013]`; global conditions versus policy in presidential success `[campellozucco2016]`. **Gaps.** Crime and political attitudes `[bateson2012]`, `[visconti2019]`.

## 9. Inequality, redistribution and social policy
**Claimed.** Democracy and the left expand social policy `[hubermustillo2008]`, `[huberstephens2012]`. **Settled.** Inequality fell in the 2000s `[lustig2013]` and cash transfers spread `[soares2010]`, `[sugiyamahunter2013]`. **Contested.** Left strength versus competition for outsiders `[garay2016]`, `[carnesmares2013]`, `[fairfieldgaray2017]`; easy versus hard redistribution `[hollandschneider2017]`. **Gaps.** Informal welfare through forbearance `[holland2016]`, `[holland2017]`, `[holland2018]` and business power over taxation `[fairfield2015]`.

## 10. Development strategies
**Claimed.** Structuralism and import substitution `[prebisch1950]`, `[hirschman1968]`; dependency `[frank1967]`, `[cardosofaletto1979]`, `[evans1979]`. **Settled.** Market reform spread through crisis and surprise `[williamson1990]`, `[stokes2001]`, `[weyland2002]`, in embedded forms `[kurtzbrooks2008]`. **Contested.** Colonial versus post-independence roots of underdevelopment `[sokoloffengerman2000]`, `[coatsworth2008]`, `[mahoney2010col]`; hierarchical capitalism `[schneider2009]`. **Gaps.** Commodity booms and Chinese finance `[kaplan2016]`, `[urdinez2016]`.

## 11. State capacity and institutional weakness
**Claimed.** War makes states `[tilly1990]`; the state reaches unevenly `[odonnell1993]`. **Settled.** Latin American war did not build states `[centeno2002]`; institutions are often weak `[levitskymurillo2009]`, `[levitskymurillo2013]`. **Contested.** Social foundations `[kurtz2013]` versus elite administrative choices `[soifer2015]`. **Gaps.** Persistent colonial effects `[dell2010]`, police weakness `[gonzalez2018]` and property rights `[holland2023]`.

## 12. Subnational politics, decentralization and federalism
**Settled.** Subnational autocracies persist inside democracies `[gibson2005]`, `[gibson2013]`, funded by transfers `[gervasoni2010]` and protected by national incumbents `[giraudy2010]`. **Contested.** Decentralization's origins `[falleti2005]`, `[eaton2006]`, its reversal `[eaton2013]` and its effect on corruption `[fan2009]`. **Gaps.** Partisan targeting of transfers `[brollo2012]`.

## 13. Crime, violence and criminal governance
**Claimed.** State-sponsored protection keeps drug markets peaceful `[snyderduranmartinez2009]`. **Settled.** Crackdowns and decapitation raise violence `[calderon2015]`, `[phillips2015]`, `[dell2015]`; alternation broke protection networks `[trejoley2017]`, `[trejoley2020]`. **Contested.** Logics of cartel-state conflict `[lessing2015]`, `[lessing2017]`; criminal governance as order `[arias2006]`, `[lessing2020]`. **Gaps.** Indigenous resistance `[leymattiace2019]`, police violence `[magaloni2020]` and voting under violence `[ley2017]`.

## 14. Courts and judicial politics
**Settled.** Judges defect strategically `[helmke2002]`; fragmentation enables independence `[riosfigueroa2007]`, `[finkel2008]`. **Contested.** Strategy versus ideas `[gonzalezocantos2014]`; distributive effects of rights litigation `[brinks2008]`, `[brinksgauri2014]`. **Gaps.** Court capture under erosion `[taylor2014]`, `[perezlinancastagnola2009]`.

## 15. Corruption and accountability
**Claimed.** Horizontal accountability is weak `[odonnell1998]`. **Settled.** Audits inform voters `[ferrazfinan2008]`, `[ferrazfinan2011]`. **Contested.** Whether information produces punishment `[chong2015]`, `[winters2013]`, `[weitzshapiro2017]`, `[boas2018]`. **Gaps.** Campaign finance and contracts `[boas2014]`.

## 16. Indigenous and ethnic politics
**Claimed.** Changing citizenship regimes sparked indigenous movements `[yashar1998]`, `[yashar2005]`. **Settled.** Movements became parties `[vancott2005]`; ethnopopulism won `[madrid2008]`, `[madrid2012]`. **Contested.** Churches versus states as drivers `[trejo2009]`; customary law as an authoritarian tool `[benton2012]`. **Gaps.** Prior consultation `[falletiriofrancos2017]` and race in Brazil `[telles2004]`.

## 17. Social movements, labor and participation
**Settled.** Labor's partisan ties shaped responses to reform `[murillo2001]`; liberalization produced protest `[arcebellinger2007]`, `[silva2009]`. **Contested.** Atomization versus mobilization `[kurtz2004]`. **Gaps.** When participatory institutions work `[goldfrank2006]`, `[wampler2008]`.

## 18. Civil-military relations and transitional justice
**Claimed.** Military professionalism produced rule `[stepan1971]`; militaries keep prerogatives `[stepan1988]`. **Settled.** Politicians eroded military power `[hunter1997]`; trials did not destabilize democracies `[sikkink2007]`, `[sikkink2011]`. **Contested.** A new militarism through policing `[floresmacias2018]`, `[pionberlinacacio2020]`. **Gaps.** Public support for militarized policing `[pionberlincarreras2017]`.

## 19. US-Latin American relations and external influences
**Claimed.** US policy rests on hierarchy `[schoultz1998]`, `[gleijeses1991]`. **Settled.** US signals shaped coups `[thyne2010]`; linkage and leverage shaped regime outcomes `[levitskyway2006]`. **Contested.** The limits of US influence `[weyland2018]`. **Gaps.** US military aid and guns `[dubenaidu2015]`, `[dube2013]`; China's rise `[floresmacias2013]`, `[kaplan2016]`.
