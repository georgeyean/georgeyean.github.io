# Technology and AI politics, c. 1980–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 269 work nodes, 17 theme nodes and 275 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[winner1980]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

The journals are *New Media & Society*, *Information, Communication & Society*, *Policy & Internet*, *JITP*, *Big Data & Society*, *Philosophy & Technology*, *AI & Society*, *Minds and Machines*, *Government Information Quarterly*, *Social Media + Society*, *Telecommunications Policy*, *Regulation & Governance* and *Nature Machine Intelligence*. ## 1. Platform power, governance and content moderation
**Claimed.** "Platform" is a political claim of neutrality `[gillespie2010]`. **Settled.** Moderation is what platforms do `[gillespie2018]`, making them new governors `[klonick2018]`. Their power rests on business models and consumer coalitions `[srnicek2017]`, `[rahmanthelen2019]`, `[culpepperthelen2020]`. **Contested.** Self-governance versus external regulation `[gorwa2019]`. AI moderation at scale `[gillespie2020]`. **Gaps.** Comparative platform regulation beyond the EU and U.S.

## 2. Algorithmic power, accountability and transparency
**Claimed.** Artifacts have politics `[winner1980]`, and code is law `[lessig1999]`. **Settled.** Algorithms are opaque for several reasons `[burrell2016]`, and transparency alone is insufficient `[anannycrawford2018]`. **Contested.** Explanation versus interpretable models `[rudin2019]`. **Gaps.** Measuring algorithmic power in political institutions.

## 3. AI ethics, principles and alignment
**Settled.** Guidelines converge on principles `[jobin2019]` but principles alone fail `[mittelstadt2019]`, `[hagendorff2020]`. **Contested.** Alignment as a technical or political problem `[russell2019]`, `[gabriel2020]`. Decolonial critiques `[mohamed2020]`. **Gaps.** Ethics of generative AI `[hagendorff2024]`, `[bender2021]`.

## 4. Algorithmic fairness, bias and discrimination
**Settled.** Fairness criteria conflict `[kleinberg2017]`, `[chouldechova2017]`, and audits reveal bias `[buolamwinigebru2018]`, `[obermeyer2019]`. **Contested.** Statistical versus structural fairness `[hoffmann2019]`, `[hedden2021]`. **Gaps.** Democratic choice among fairness definitions `[wong2020]`.

## 5. Surveillance, datafication and privacy
**Claimed.** Privacy is contextual integrity `[nissenbaum2010]`. **Settled.** Surveillance capitalism `[zuboff2019]` and data colonialism `[couldrymejias2019]` frame the political economy of data. **Contested.** Individual privacy choices `[acquisti2015]`, `[obar2020]`. **Gaps.** State versus corporate surveillance compared.

## 6. Digital authoritarianism, censorship and propaganda
**Claimed.** Liberation technology `[diamond2010]` versus the net delusion `[morozov2011]`. **Settled.** Autocrats censor collective action `[kingpanroberts2013]`, flood information `[king2017]`, `[roberts2018]` and use AI surveillance `[feldstein2019]`, `[xu2021]`. **Contested.** Informational autocracy `[guriev2019]` versus repression. **Gaps.** Export of surveillance AI `[beraja2023]`.

## 7. Misinformation, bots, deepfakes and computational propaganda
**Settled.** False news spreads fast `[vosoughi2018]`, but exposure is concentrated `[allcottgentzkow2017]`, `[guess2019]`. **Contested.** Inattention versus partisanship `[pennycookrand2019]`, `[osmundsen2021]`. Media ecosystems over technology `[benkler2018]`. **Gaps.** Deepfakes and generative AI `[vaccarichadwick2020]`.

## 8. Social media, polarization and the public sphere
**Contested.** Echo chambers `[sunstein2001]`, `[pariser2011]` versus moderate diets `[flaxman2016]`, `[guess2021]`, `[duboisblank2018]`. Feed algorithms change exposure but not attitudes `[guess2023]`. Social media use has welfare and polarization effects `[allcott2020]`. **Gaps.** Platforms after 2023 and closed messaging apps.

## 9. Digital activism and participation
**Settled.** Connective action `[bennettsegerberg2012]` and social media raise protest participation `[tufekciwilson2012]`, `[enikolopov2020]`. **Contested.** Networked protest's fragility `[tufekci2017]`. **Gaps.** Platform governance as a constraint on activism.

## 10. Digital campaigns and political communication
**Settled.** The hybrid media system `[chadwick2013]`. Tech firms shape campaigns `[kreissmcgregor2018]`. **Gaps.** AI-generated campaign content.

## 11. Digital divides and inequality
**Settled.** Divides moved from access to skills and use `[hargittai2002]`, `[vandijk2005]`, `[vandeursen2014]`. **Gaps.** Algorithm awareness as a new divide `[gran2021]`.

## 12. Automation, work and the political economy of AI
**Settled.** Robots reduce local employment `[acemoglurestrepo2020]`, and exposed workers move toward the radical right `[anelli2021]`, `[kurer2020]`. **Contested.** Complementarity `[autor2015]` versus displacement `[freyosborne2017]`. Generative AI productivity `[brynjolfsson2025]`. **Gaps.** Attitudes toward AI as trade-like politics `[magistro2025]`. This is close to your work.

## 13. Digital government and AI in the public sector
**Settled.** Discretion moves from street level to system level `[bovenszouridis2002]`, `[youngbullock2019]`. **Contested.** Automated welfare `[eubanks2018]`. **Gaps.** Citizen trust in AI decisions `[ingrams2022]`.

## 14. Regulating technology and AI governance
**Settled.** The Brussels effect `[bradford2020]` and competing digital empires `[bradford2023]`. **Contested.** Soft ethics versus hard law `[floridi2018ptech]`, `[laux2024]`. **Gaps.** Enforcement of the EU AI Act.

## 15. Cyber conflict, security and military AI
**Contested.** Cyber war will not take place `[rid2012]` versus a cyber revolution `[kello2013]`. **Gaps.** Military AI and judgment `[goldfarb2022]`, `[horowitz2018]`.

## 16. Tech geopolitics
**Contested.** Innovation `[kennedylim2018]` versus diffusion `[ding2024]` as the key to rivalry. Weaponized interdependence `[farrell2019]`. **Gaps.** Chips and AI export controls.

## 17. Computational social science and LLMs
**Settled.** Text as data `[grimmerstewart2013]`. LLMs annotate well `[gilardi2023]`. **Contested.** Bias from predicted variables `[egami2025]` and simulated samples `[argyle2023]`. **Gaps.** Validated LLM measurement of political texts `[benoit2026]`. This is your methods niche.
