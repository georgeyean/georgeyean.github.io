# Political theory, c. 1755–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 407 work nodes, 17 theme nodes and 449 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what is broadly accepted → what is contested → gaps and openings**. In normative theory "settled" means rough consensus on the terms of debate, not empirical proof. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[rawls1971]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Your library:** 38 works come from `papers.json`, mostly the *Gov Theory* reading list (Rawls, Anderson, Cohen, Shelby, Fricker, Fraser, Taylor, Okin, Haslanger, Crenshaw, Waldron, Zacka and others). Many of those entries are PDF excerpts, so I supplied their bibliographic details by hand and checked them in Crossref.
- **Shared works:** 8 works already in the other maps are reused, including Mansbridge on descriptive representation, Beitz on human rights, Mudde on populism and Achen & Bartels.
- **Top-cited articles:** 266 come from the 400 most-cited 2006–2026 articles in fourteen journals: *Political Theory*, *Journal of Political Philosophy*, *Philosophy & Public Affairs*, *Ethics*, *European Journal of Political Theory*, *Contemporary Political Theory*, *CRISPP*, *Constellations*, *Politics, Philosophy & Economics*, *Hypatia*, *Res Publica*, *The Review of Politics*, *Polity* and *Modern Intellectual History*. A few theory articles from *APSR*, *JOP*, the *Annual Review* and *Perspectives on Politics* are included too.
- **Anchors:** 95 books and classic articles, from Rousseau and Berlin through Rawls, Habermas, Young, Pettit and recent books such as Getachew and Lafont.

One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries, not re-read this session. Political theory is book-centered, so citation counts of articles understate its core. The anchors carry much of the weight here.

## 1. Distributive justice and equality

**Claimed.** Justice as fairness `[rawls1971]` set the agenda. Libertarians answered with entitlement theory `[nozick1974]`, and Sen asked "equality of what?" `[sen1980]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Luck egalitarianism `[dworkin1981]`, `[cohen1989]`, `[arneson1989]` met a strong relational challenge `[anderson1999]`, `[scheffler2003]`. Equality now has several distinct grounds `[scanlon2018]`, `[oneill2008]`.

**Contested.**
- Equality, priority or sufficiency `[frankfurt1987]`, `[casal2007]`, `[otsukavoorhoeve2009]`.
- Capabilities versus resources `[nussbaum2000]`, `[nelson2008]`.
- Incentives and desert `[cohen1992]`, `[mankiw2013]`.

**Openings.** Limitarianism `[robeyns2022]` and opportunity structures `[fishkin2014]` connect justice to concentrated wealth and political power. That link to IPE and inequality research is underdeveloped.

## 2. Ideal and nonideal theory, realism and method

**Claimed.** Ideal theory guides practice `[rawls1971]`. Critics call it ideology `[mills2005]` and argue for comparative assessment `[sen2006]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Realism is a major research program `[williams2005]`, `[geuss2008]`, `[galston2010]`, with legitimacy treated as prior to justice. Feasibility has become an object of analysis `[lawfordsmith2013]`.

**Contested.** Whether ideal theory is needed for comparison `[simmons2010]`, `[valentini2009]`. Whether unrealistic standards are acceptable `[estlund2014]`. Whether there is a distinctively political normativity `[leadermaynard2018]`.

**Openings.** Grounded normative theory `[ackerly2021]` and interpretive method `[taylor1971]`, `[macintyre1971]` invite theory that draws on empirical political science. That suits your methods.

## 3. Legitimacy, authority and public reason

**Claimed.** Under reasonable pluralism, legitimacy needs public reason `[rawls1993]`, `[rawls1997]`. Authority serves reasons `[raz1986]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Debate has shifted to relational justifications of democracy: no one should rule over others `[kolodny2014b]`, `[viehoff2014]`, and each person has a right to justification `[forst2012]`.

**Contested.** Consensus or convergence `[gaus2011]`, `[quong2011]`. Courts versus legislatures `[waldron2006]`. How to treat religion `[laborde2017]`.

**Openings.** Institutions are understudied in legitimacy theory `[waldron2013]`, `[zacka2017]`.

## 4. Deliberative democracy and democratic innovations

**Claimed.** Legitimacy flows from reasoned deliberation among equals `[cohen1989delib]`, `[gutmannthompson1996]`, `[habermas1996]`.

**Broadly accepted.** The systemic turn `[parkinsonmansbridge2012]`, `[owensmith2015]` and evidence that people want to deliberate `[neblo2010]`. Deliberation can include self-interest `[mansbridge2010]`.

**Contested.** Mini-publics and sortition as shortcuts `[lafont2015]`, `[lafont2020]` versus lottocracy `[guerrero2014]`, `[landemore2020]`. Agonism as a rival `[mouffe2000]`, `[knops2007]`.

**Openings.** Deliberation under inequality `[karpowitz2012]`, `[parthasarathy2019]` and text-as-data study of real deliberation fit your methods.

## 5. Representation

**Claimed.** Substantive acting-for is the core of representation `[pitkin1967]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Representation has many forms `[mansbridge2003]`, `[rehfeld2009]`. Descriptive representation matters in specific contexts `[mansbridge1999]`.

**Contested.** The constructivist turn `[saward2006]`, `[disch2011]` and its normative limits `[disch2015]`. Lottery versus election `[manin1997]`, `[landapevnick2021]`.

**Openings.** Representing future generations `[thompson2010]` and transnational constituencies.

## 6. Epistemic democracy, expertise and epistemic injustice

**Claimed.** Democracy has epistemic value `[estlund2008]`, `[landemore2013]`, `[anderson2006]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Epistemic injustice is a core concept `[fricker2007]`, `[dotson2011]`, `[pohlhaus2012]`.

**Contested.** Epistocracy `[brennan2016]` versus its critics `[hannon2022]`. The role of experts `[holstmolander2019]`.

**Openings.** Propaganda and misinformation `[stanley2015]` link to the American politics map.

## 7. Republicanism, freedom and domination

**Claimed.** Freedom is non-domination `[pettit1997]`, `[skinner1998]`, distinct from Berlin's concepts `[berlin1958]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Republicanism now reaches into economic life `[gourevitch2013]`, `[anderson2017]`.

**Contested.** Whether non-domination suffices `[markell2008]`. Structural versus interpersonal domination `[gadeke2020]`. Socialist republicanism `[oshea2020]`.

**Openings.** Algorithmic domination `[muldoonraekstad2023]` and platform labor.

## 8. Global justice and human rights

**Claimed.** Cosmopolitan justice `[beitz1979]`, `[pogge2002]` versus statism `[rawls1999]`, `[nagel2005]`.

**Broadly accepted.** A practical conception of human rights `[beitz2009]`.

**Contested.** The scope of justice:
- coercion `[nagel2005]`;
- reciprocity `[sangiovanni2007]`;
- critics of both `[abizadeh2007]`, `[cohensabel2006]`.

**Openings.** Structural injustice `[young2011]` and resource justice `[wenar2008]` link directly to IPE.

## 9. Migration, borders and the boundary problem

**Claimed.** Open borders `[carens1987]` versus communal closure `[walzer1983]`.

**Broadly accepted.** The boundary problem is central to democratic theory `[goodin2007]`.

**Contested.** Border coercion `[abizadeh2008]`, `[miller2010]`. The right to exclude `[wellman2008]`, `[blake2013]`.

**Openings.** Temporary migrants `[baubock2011]` and refugee burden sharing `[gibney2015]`.

## 10. Race, colonialism and racial justice

**Claimed.** The racial contract `[mills1997]`. Liberalism has been tied to empire `[mehta1999]`, `[pitts2005]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Race and empire are central to political theory `[getachew2019]`, `[coulthard2014]`.

**Contested.** Integration `[anderson2010]` versus the ghetto's claims `[shelby2016]`.

**Openings.** Racial capitalism `[issar2021]` and the theory of Black Lives Matter `[hooker2016]`.

## 11. Feminist theory, gender and intersectionality

**Claimed.** The sexual contract `[pateman1988]` and the family as part of justice `[okin1989]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Intersectionality `[crenshaw1991]`, `[collins1990]` and ameliorative analysis `[haslanger2000]`.

**Contested.** Trans inclusion `[jenkins2016]`, `[bettcher2007]`. Carceral feminism `[terwiel2020]`.

**Openings.** Care as a democratic practice `[woodly2021]`.

## 12. Multiculturalism, recognition and identity

**Claimed.** The politics of recognition `[taylor1992]` and liberal minority rights `[kymlicka1995]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Recognition and redistribution are both central `[fraser1995]`, `[fraserhonneth2003]`.

**Contested.** Multiculturalism versus equality `[barry2001]` and gender `[okin1999]`. Recognition as colonial `[coulthard2007]`.

**Openings.** Identity critique `[appiah2018]` and religion `[laborde2017]`.

## 13. History of political thought and comparative political theory

**Claimed.** Contextualism `[skinner1969]` and the Atlantic republican tradition `[pocock1975]`.

**Broadly accepted.** History of thought has globalized `[march2009]`, `[getachew2019]`.

**Contested.** What comparative political theory is for `[williamswarren2014]`.

**Openings.** Confucian and Indian thought `[kimsungmoon2011]`, `[mehta2010]`.

## 14. Critical theory, power and radical democracy

**Claimed.** Three dimensions of power `[lukes1974]` and disciplinary power `[foucault1977]`.

**Broadly accepted.** The neoliberalism critique `[brown2015]`.

**Contested.** Agonism versus deliberation `[mouffe2000]`. Rancière versus Arendt `[schaap2011]`.

**Openings.** Prefigurative politics `[raekstad2018]`.

## 15. Populism and the people

**Claimed.** Populism as thin ideology `[mudde2004]` or as a political logic `[laclau2005]`.

**Broadly accepted.** Populism is antipluralist `[muller2016]`.

**Contested.** Populism as democratic corrective `[vergara2020]` versus a disfigurement of democracy `[urbinati2019book]`.

**Openings.** Technocracy and populism `[bickertonaccetti2017]`.

## 16. Work, markets, corporations and property

**Broadly accepted.** Workplaces are sites of government `[anderson2017]`.

**Contested.** Workplace democracy `[landemoreferreras2016]` and the corporation's status `[ciepley2013]`.

**Openings.** The gig economy `[muldoonraekstad2023]`.

## 17. Climate, future generations and nonhuman justice

**Broadly accepted.** Climate burdens should be shared `[caney2010]`.

**Contested.** Historical emissions `[meyerroser2010]` and individual duties `[kagan2011]`, `[nefsky2011]`.
