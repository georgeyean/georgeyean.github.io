# Middle Eastern politics, c. 1950–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 344 work nodes, 19 theme nodes and 406 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[bellin2004]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 43 works already in other maps are reused. Most come from the comparative politics map (authoritarian institutions, competitive authoritarianism, backsliding, the Arab uprisings) and the environmental and political economy maps (the oil curse). Others come from security and IR (civil war, terrorism and voting in Israel, alliances, the Iraq war), technology (digital media and the uprisings), psychology (emotions in the uprisings) and REP (Gulf migration).
- **Top-cited articles:** 131 come from the most-cited 2006–2026 articles in six newly fetched journals: the *International Journal of Middle East Studies*, *Middle East Journal*, *Mediterranean Politics*, *Democratization*, the *British Journal of Middle Eastern Studies* and *Middle Eastern Studies*. Region-related articles in the *Journal of Democracy*, *Comparative Politics*, *World Politics*, APSR, JOP, BJPS, CPS, ARPS, *Perspectives on Politics*, JCR, JPR, IO, ISQ, *International Security*, *Security Studies*, JPE, QJE, *Public Choice*, *Socio-Economic Review* and the *International Migration Review* were added too.
- **Anchors:** 126 books and classic articles (88 of them books), from Mahdavy's rentier state and Kerr's *Arab Cold War* to Bellin, Brownlee, Lust-Okar, Schwedler, Wickham, Blaydes, Herb and Yom & Gause.

Relations are judgments of how each work's argument relates to the other's, and every relation runs from the later work to the earlier one. One-line claims are my summaries.

## 1. Authoritarian resilience and the coercive state
**Claimed.** Middle Eastern states should be explained by how they were formed, not by culture `[anderson1987]`; regimes survive by controlled liberalization `[brumberg2002]` and by a coercive apparatus with the will and capacity to repress `[bellin2004]`. **Settled.** Ruling parties and institutions bind elites and lengthen rule `[brownlee2007book]`, `[gandhiprzeworski2007]`, `[svolik2012book]`; regimes "upgrade" by managing civil society and new coalitions `[heydemann2007]`, `[king2009]`. **Contested.** Whether the study of "persistence" asked the wrong questions `[anderson2006arps]`, `[hinnebusch2006]`, and whether 2011 refuted the robustness thesis `[gause2011]`, `[bellin2012]`. **Gaps.** Post-2013 recentralized militarized rule `[rutherford2018]`, `[josuaedel2021]`; how archives show surveillance and compliance at work `[blaydes2018iraq]`, `[blaydes2020]`.

## 2. Monarchies and ruling bargains
**Claimed.** Monarchies are modern state-building regimes `[anderson1991]`; dynastic family rule makes them durable `[herb1999]`. **Settled.** Monarchs stand above parties and divide oppositions `[lucas2004]`; oil rents underwrite ruling bargains `[gause1994]`, `[crystal1990]`. **Contested.** What saved the monarchies in 2011: coalitions, rents and patrons `[yomgause2012]`, political culture `[menaldo2012]`, or combinations `[bank2013]`. **Gaps.** Renegotiating the bargain as rents shrink `[gengler2016]`, `[yom2014]`, and repression of online dissent `[pansiegel2019]`.

## 3. Rentier states, oil and political economy
**Claimed.** States living on external rents need not tax and so need not represent `[mahdavy1970]`, `[beblawiluciani1987]`. **Settled.** Oil is linked to durable autocracy, particularly after the 1970s nationalizations `[ross2001]`, `[smith2004]`, `[andersenross2013]`, `[wrightfrantzgeddes2013]`. **Contested.** Whether the curse is real `[habermenaldo2011]`, `[herb2005]`, `[ross2012]`; whether it runs through fiscal rents or oil's material politics `[mitchell2011]`; and whether rentier states can build efficient institutions `[hertog2010]`, `[hertog2010book]`. **Gaps.** Whether citizens actually hold a "rentier mentality" `[hertog2020]`, `[gengler2021]`, and identity-based allocation of rents `[gengler2015]`.

## 4. Elections, parties and clientelism under autocracy
**Claimed.** Regimes structure opposition to keep it divided `[lustokar2005]`, `[albrecht2005]`. **Settled.** Elections are competitions for patronage that stabilize autocracy `[lustokar2006]`, `[lust2009]`, `[blaydes2011]`, and voters reward service delivery `[demiguel2015]`. **Contested.** Whether hybrid regimes still raise later chances of democracy `[brownlee2009]`. **Gaps.** Vote prices and communal monopoly `[corstange2012]`, `[corstange2016]`.

## 5. The Arab uprisings and their aftermath
**Claimed.** Revolutions surprise because dissent is hidden `[kuran1991]`. **Settled.** Digital media and emotions helped mobilize `[howardhussain2011]`, `[pearlman2013]`, `[breuer2014]`, and the uprisings spread by emulation `[weyland2012]`, `[hale2013]`. **Contested.** Who mobilized and why: brokers `[clarke2014]`, peripheral users `[steinertthrelkeld2017]`, organizers `[clarkekocak2018]`; and what predicted outcomes, from oil and hereditary succession `[brownlee2015]` to the lack of revolutionary programs `[bayat2017]`. **Gaps.** The return of repression `[josuaedel2021]` and disillusion after founding elections `[grewalmonroe2019]`.

## 6. Islamist movements and inclusion-moderation
**Claimed.** Islamists mobilize through networks and moral calls `[wickham2002]`, `[clark2004]`; religious parties face commitment problems `[kalyvas2000]`. **Settled.** Inclusion moderated some Islamists on some issues `[schwedler2006]`, `[clark2006]`, `[schwedler2011]`. **Contested.** Moderation through exclusion `[cavatortamerone2013]`, `[hamid2014]`; reversal in power `[bashirov2018]`, `[somer2016]`; and the sources and size of the Islamist electoral advantage `[cammettluong2014]`, `[masoud2014]`, `[brooke2019]`, `[kurzmannaqvi2010]`. **Gaps.** Islamists after 2013 and the transnational jihadist turn `[hegghammer2010]`, `[walter2017]`.

## 7. Religion, secularism and democracy
**Claimed.** Islam is linked to authoritarianism `[fish2002]`. **Settled.** The deficit is Arab, not Muslim `[stepanrobertson2003]`, and personal religiosity does not reduce democratic support `[tessler2002]`, `[ciftci2010]`. **Contested.** The historical roots: Islamic law `[kuran2011]`, military-state legacies `[blaydeschaney2013]`, or an ulema-state alliance `[kuru2019]`. **Gaps.** Twin tolerations in practice `[stepan2000]`, `[stepan2012]`, and religious framing as a lever for change `[masoud2016]`, `[clingingsmith2009]`.

## 8. Civil-military relations and coups
**Claimed.** Rulers coup-proof with kin units and parallel forces `[quinlivan1999]`. **Settled.** Coup-proofing lowers battlefield effectiveness `[talmadge2013]`, `[talmadge2015]`, and the army's choice decided the 2011 revolts `[barany2011]`, `[nepstad2013]`. **Contested.** Whether coup-proofing predicts loyalty `[makara2013]`, `[albrecht2014]`. **Gaps.** Military economic empires `[bounassif2013]`, `[forozan2017]` and coups against new democracies `[esengumuscu2017jod]`.

## 9. Sectarianism, ethnicity and identity
**Claimed.** Sectarianism is modern and political `[makdisi2000]`, or a deep regional cleavage `[nasr2006]`. **Settled.** Elites sectarianize conflict `[matthiesen2013]`, `[hashemipostel2017]`, and sectarian parties target welfare strategically `[cammettissar2010]`, `[cammett2014]`. **Contested.** Whether "sectarianism" is a useful concept `[haddad2017]`. **Gaps.** Reducing sectarian hostility `[siegelbadaan2020]` and Kurdish exclusion and mobilization `[tezcurgurses2017]`.

## 10. Civil wars, insurgency and intervention
**Claimed.** Violence follows control `[kalyvas2006]` and opportunity `[fearonlaitin2003]`. **Settled.** Small aid projects and information reduced violence in Iraq `[berman2011hearts]`, where the surge worked with the Awakening `[biddle2012]`. **Contested.** Climate's role in Syria `[kelley2015]`, `[selby2017]`, `[ashobradovich2019]`, `[dechatel2014]`; whether intervention protects civilians `[kuperman2013]`. **Gaps.** Rebel governance `[revkin2020]` and regional proxy wars `[lynch2016]`, `[phillips2016]`.

## 11. The Israeli-Palestinian conflict
**Claimed.** Israel controls its Arab minority `[lustick1980]`. **Settled.** Violence and threat shift Israeli votes rightward `[berrebi2008]`, `[getmansky2014]`. **Contested.** How to characterize the Israeli regime `[smooha1997]`, `[yiftachel2006]`; spoiling versus outbidding `[kyddwalter2002]`, `[bloom2004]`, `[pearlman2011]`. **Gaps.** Barriers to compromise `[halperin2008]`, `[maoz2011]`, `[grossman2015]` and Israel's democratic crisis `[gidron2023]`.

## 12. Iran
**Claimed.** Rentier autonomy and Shi'a networks made 1979 `[skocpol1982]`. **Settled.** Uneven development strained the Pahlavi state `[abrahamian1982]`. **Contested.** Structure versus perception in the revolution `[kurzman2004]`, `[arjomand1988]`. **Gaps.** Welfare and the semi-state economy `[harris2017]`, `[harris2013]`, electoral reformism `[kadivar2020]` and the Revolutionary Guards `[forozan2017]`.

## 13. Turkey and democratic backsliding
**Claimed.** Turkish politics turns on a center-periphery cleavage `[mardin1973]`. **Settled.** Turkey became competitive authoritarian `[esengumuscu2016]`, `[levitskyway2020]`. **Contested.** Whether the AKP moderated or conquered the state `[somer2016]`, `[bashirov2018]`, `[gumuscusert2009]`. **Gaps.** Polarized partisans' tolerance of autocratization `[laebensozturk2020]`, `[svolik2019]`, and opposition coordination `[selcuk2020]`.

## 14. State formation and colonial legacies
**Claimed.** State formation explains regime variety `[anderson1986]`, `[anderson1987]`. **Settled.** Colonial and external intervention shaped weak but fierce states `[lustick1997]`, `[ayubi1995]`, `[owen1992]`. **Contested.** Discipline versus capacity accounts of colonial power `[mitchell1988]`. **Gaps.** Long-run institutions `[blaydes2017]`, `[kuran2011]` and the colonial roots of labor regimes `[alshehabi2019]`.

## 15. Public opinion and the Arab Barometer
**Settled.** Arabs broadly support democracy `[jamaltessler2008]`, `[tessler2012]`. **Contested.** What shapes doubts: performance `[benstead2014]`, US hegemony `[jamal2012]`, or clientelist associational life `[jamal2007]`. **Gaps.** Opinion on foreign policy `[furialucas2006]`, `[blaydeslinzer2012]`.

## 16. Gender and politics
**Claimed.** Patriarchy is bargained with `[kandiyoti1988]`; state-kin ties shape women's rights `[charrad2001]`. **Contested.** Oil versus Islam `[ross2008]`, `[fish2002]`, `[donnorussett2004]`. **Gaps.** Quotas and autocratic legitimacy `[bushgao2017]`, `[tripp2019]` and candidate evaluation `[benstead2015]`.

## 17. Migration, refugees and labor
**Settled.** Kafala gives employers monopsony power `[naidu2016]`. **Contested.** Host-state refugee policy `[turner2015]`, `[fakhoury2017]`, `[tsourapas2019]`. **Gaps.** Return `[alrababah2023]` and host attitudes `[alrababah2020]`, `[altindag2020]`.

## 18. Great powers and regional order
**Claimed.** Inter-Arab politics is a contest over legitimacy `[kerr1971]`, `[barnett1998]`; states balance threats `[walt1987]` and omnibalance `[david1991]`. **Contested.** Identity versus threat `[valbjornbank2012]`, `[gause2014]`. **Gaps.** Patronage as a source of fragility `[yom2016]`, `[brownlee2012]`.

## 19. Crony capitalism and economic reform
**Settled.** Reform was captured by networks of privilege `[heydemann2004]`, `[haddad2012]`, `[rijkers2017]`, `[diwan2019]`. **Contested.** Whether state-dependent capital supports democracy `[bellin2002]`. **Gaps.** Segmented labor markets `[hertog2020ser]` and the economic roots of 2011 `[malikawadallah2013]`, `[springborg2011]`.
