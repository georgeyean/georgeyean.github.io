# Chinese politics, c. 1986–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 183 work nodes, 16 theme nodes and 183 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[lieberthal1988]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Your library:** the China-related entries in `papers.json` (from the *Gov Iain China*, *China Econ Poli*, *China Power* and *China Growth* groups, among others). Some have full analyses; for the others I used their abstracts.
- **Shared works:** China works already in the IPE, security, IR and comparative maps (King, Pan & Roberts; Shih; Truex; Weiss; Johnston; Pearson, Rithmire & Tsai) are imported unchanged.
- **Top-cited articles:** 108 come from the 400 most-cited 2006–2026 articles in six China-studies journals (*The China Quarterly*, *The China Journal*, *Journal of Contemporary China*, *Modern China*, *Journal of Chinese Political Science*, *Journal of East Asian Studies*), plus China-related articles in the 21 political science journals used for the other maps. Their metadata comes from Crossref.
- **Anchors:** 23 books and classic articles (Walder, Lieberthal & Oksenberg, Shirk, Oi, Naughton, O'Brien & Li, Lee, Brady, Pei, Dickson, Roberts, Greitens). All were found in Crossref, mostly through published reviews.

One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries, not re-read this session. Sociology-heavy topics (family, education, health, gender) were filtered out to keep the focus on politics and political economy.

## 1. Elite politics, factions and cadre promotion

**Claimed.** The party governs decentralized localities through cadre management `[landry2008]`, and promotion tournaments reward growth `[lizhou2005]`. Institutionalized succession and meritocratic promotion explain regime resilience `[nathan2003]`.

**Settled.** Promotion matters for local behavior: jurisdictional competition raises extraction `[luelandry2014]`, leaders time policy around promotion calendars `[chenzhang2021]`, and evaluation systems shape cadre strategies `[heberer2013]` but are manipulated `[gao2015]`. Career incentives drove Great Leap radicalism `[kungchen2011]`.

**Contested.** Growth or connections:
- factional ties dominate at the top `[shih2012]`, `[choi2012]`;
- performance matters at lower levels `[landry2017]`, `[zuo2015]`;
- patronage and performance interact `[jiang2018]`.

Anticorruption is politicized factional competition `[zhuzhang2016]`. Factional conflict also drives finance `[shih2008]`.

**Gaps.** Almost all promotion evidence predates 2015. Under Xi, loyalty criteria, purges and term-limit removal may have broken the growth-tournament model. Personnel data after 2015, plus text of official evaluations coded with validated LLMs, could test whether loyalty replaced performance.

## 2. Central–local relations, decentralization and the policy process

**Claimed.** Policy making is fragmented authoritarianism, with bureaucratic bargaining `[lieberthal1988]`. Fiscal decentralization created local state corporatism `[oi1992]`, `[oi1999]` and market-preserving federalism `[montinola1995]`.

**Settled.** Experimentation under hierarchy is China's distinctive policy process `[heilmann2008cj]`, `[heilmann2008]`, with Maoist roots `[heilmannperry2011]` and directed improvisation `[ang2016]`. Local governments collude to manage mandates `[zhou2010]`. The system is regionally decentralized authoritarianism `[xu2011]`.

**Contested.** Whether decentralization caused growth `[caitreisman2006]`. Whether pluralization (fragmented authoritarianism 2.0 `[mertha2009]`) survived recentralization under Xi `[kostkanahm2017]`. Central and local governments responded differently to WTO entry `[tan2020]`.

**Gaps.** Xi-era recentralization is the field's central open question: leading small groups, central inspection teams, campaigns and "top-level design". The policy-process literature built on the 1990s–2000s needs a recentralized update with systematic data.

## 3. Responsiveness, consultation and participation

**Settled.** The regime is responsive in measurable ways:
- local officials answer citizen requests, especially those signaling collective action `[chenpanxu2016]`;
- officials provide constituency service `[distelhorst2017]`;
- consultative institutions give citizens voice `[hethogersen2010]`, `[fishkin2010]`;
- the NPC co-opts and represents `[truex2014]`, `[truex2016book]`.

Informal solidary groups `[tsai2007]` and lineages `[xuyao2015]` enable local accountability. Consultative authoritarianism extends to civil society `[teets2013]`.

**Contested.** How far responsiveness reaches. Receptivity is conditional on threat `[meng2017]`, organized participation was repressed under Xi `[fudistelhorst2018]`, and village elections did not lead to democracy `[obrienhan2009]`.

**Gaps.** Responsiveness research relied on field experiments that are now much harder to run in China. New designs that use public government data (portal replies, official accounts) analyzed at scale with text methods are the way forward.

## 4. Censorship, propaganda and digital control

**Settled.** Censorship targets collective action, not criticism `[kingpanroberts2013]`. The state fabricates posts to distract `[king2017]`, and porous censorship works through friction and flooding `[roberts2018]`. Some watchdog reporting is tolerated to gather information `[lorentzen2014]`. Propaganda was modernized after 1989 `[brady2008]`, `[shambaugh2007]`, made persuasive through commercial media `[stockmann2011]`, and upgraded digitally `[creemers2016]`, with netizens enlisted as collaborators `[repnikova2018]`, `[han2015]`.

**Contested.** How censorship varies by issue and time `[tai2014]`, as in nationalist crises `[cairns2016]`. Whether international knowledge undercuts propaganda `[huang2015]`. Whether "Autocracy 2.0" is a stable new equilibrium of information control `[yang2024]`.

**Gaps.**
- *Generative AI.* How the party uses LLMs for propaganda, censorship and public-opinion monitoring, and how domestic models are aligned to party lines, is untested empirically.
- *Propaganda abroad.* State media persuades foreign audiences `[mattingly2025]`, and Confucius Institutes `[hartig2011]` are a soft-power tool. Systematic evidence on which outlets and messages work is thin.

## 5. Protest, petitions and labor contention

**Settled.** Protesters use the regime's own laws and commitments (rightful resistance `[obrienli2006]`). Workers protest within the law `[lee2007]`. Petitioning creates contentious bargaining `[chen2012]`. Small protests are tolerated as information `[lorentzen2013]`. Central-local divisions shape outcomes `[cai2008bjps]`, `[cai2008cq]`. Petitioners lose trust as they move up the hierarchy `[li2008]`. Labor contention moved from bargaining by riot toward party-led collective bargaining `[channgai2009]`, `[chanhui2013]`, and activists disguise collective action `[fu2016]`. Environmental protest involves new middle-class actors `[steinhardtwu2016]`.

**Gap.** Protest data after 2015 are scarce because monitoring sources were shut down. Whether contention declined, changed form or went unrecorded is a measurement problem as much as a substantive one.

## 6. Repression, surveillance and the security state

**Settled.** Spending on stability maintenance and security institutions expanded dramatically `[wangminzner2015]`. Repression uses protesters' social ties `[dengobrien2013]` and graduated controls on organizations `[kanghan2008]`. Xinjiang moved to preventive repression `[greitens2020]` through surveillance and ethnic sorting `[leibold2019]`. Coercive institutions are designed against elite or mass threats `[greitens2016]`.

**Gap.** The export of surveillance technology and policing models to other autocracies links this section to the comparative and IPE maps. Systematic data on recipients and effects are limited.

## 7. Regime resilience, legitimacy and political trust

**Claimed.** Institutionalization made the regime resilient `[nathan2003]`. The opposing view is that China is trapped in a predatory partial transition `[pei2006]`.

**Settled.** Measured trust is high for the center and drops steeply for local government `[li2016]`. The regime survives through a mix of repression, public goods, co-optation and responsiveness, with real popular support `[dickson2016]`. Performance legitimacy is fragile `[zhu2011]`. Public opinion has a coherent ideological structure `[panxu2018]`.

**Contested.** How much measured support reflects preference falsification `[jiangyang2016]`. Whether a "China model" exists `[kennedy2010]`. Revolutionary legacies shape governance `[perry2007]`, and Cultural Revolution violence lowers trust today `[wang2019]`.

**Gap.** Survey-based regime support research faces growing access limits and falsification problems. List experiments, behavioral measures and online text would help triangulate.

## 8. Nationalism and foreign-policy opinion

**Settled.** Patriotic education built humiliation-centered nationalism after 1989 `[wang2012]`. Beijing allows or stops nationalist protest to signal resolve or reassurance `[weiss2013]`, `[weiss2014]`, and citizens punish backing down `[weissdafoe2019]`, though face-saving helps `[quek2018]`.

**Contested.** Whether nationalism is rising: Beijing survey data show it is not `[johnston2017]`, yet the public is hawkish `[weiss2019jcc]`. Nationalism and patriotism have different effects `[gries2011]`. Whether the state now follows nationalism rather than controls it `[zhao2013]`. Whether China's 2010s assertiveness was new `[johnston2013]`.

**Gap.** Nationalism research relies on urban or online samples. Nationally representative evidence after 2016, and evidence on how nationalism shapes elite decisions rather than mass opinion, is thin.

## 9. Political economy: growth, state capitalism and business

**Claimed.** Gradual dual-track reform grew China out of the plan `[naughton2007]`, through elite politics `[shirk1993]`. Rural entrepreneurship drove early growth, then a statist turn followed `[huang2008]`.

**Settled.** Private firms grew by replacing less productive but capital-favored SOEs `[song2011]`. The party co-opted entrepreneurs, who support the regime `[dickson2007]`, `[chendickson2008]`, `[ktsai2007]`, and it controls business groups through personnel `[brodsgaard2012]`. Globalization reformed labor relations `[gallagher2005]`. Land revenue drives urbanization `[ong2014]`. Growth was fastest during state withdrawal `[malesky2014]`.

**Contested.** Sino-capitalism `[mcnally2012]` and party-state capitalism `[pearson2022]`, `[rithmire2023]` challenge the idea that China converges to market capitalism. SOEs project state power abroad but pursue commercial goals `[stone2021]`. Connections and courts are complements `[angjia2014]`.

**Gaps.** Party-state capitalism is documented qualitatively. Firm-level measures of party control (party cells, state funds, golden shares) linked to firm behavior at home and abroad would turn it into a testable variable. This links to geoeconomics in the IPE map and to your dual-use work.

## 10. Corruption, anti-corruption and law

Corruption rose with growth but was contained `[wedeman2012]`, or it decayed into crony capitalism `[pei2016]`. Hong Kong escaped a corruption equilibrium through credible reform `[manion2004]`. Bribery runs through guanxi `[li2010]`. Multinationals create rents `[zhu2016]`. Xi's campaign is partly factional `[zhuzhang2016]`. Imperial size created principal-agent problems `[sng2014]`.

**Gap.** The post-2012 campaign is the largest in the regime's history. Its effects on bureaucratic behavior (risk aversion, "lazy governance"), growth and investment are only beginning to be measured.

## 11. Civil society and NGOs

Consultative authoritarianism allows controlled civil society `[teets2013]`. Local corporatist states shape NGO relations `[hsuhasmath2014]`. Registration rules are a control tool `[hildebrandt2011]`, alongside graduated controls `[kanghan2008]`. The Sichuan earthquake spurred grassroots associations `[shieh2011]`. Policy entrepreneurs entered the policy process `[mertha2009]`.

**Gap.** The 2017 Overseas NGO Law and the post-2015 crackdown changed the field. Research after 2017 on what civil society remains, and how it operates, is limited.

## 12. Environmental governance

Recentralization strengthened environmental monitoring and enforcement `[kostkanahm2017]`, `[vanrooij2017]`. Local leaders' short time horizons undermine authoritarian environmentalism `[eatonkostka2014]`, and decentralization creates blame politics `[ran2017]`. Implementation bundles national and local interests `[kostkahobbs2012]`. Industrial giants undermine transparency `[lorentzen2014jop]`.

**Gap.** China's industrial policy for clean energy (solar, batteries, EVs) is now a central IPE topic (the second China shock). Its domestic politics—which coalitions and localities drive it, and how it links to environmental governance—is understudied.

## 13. Imperial and Maoist legacies

Imperial rulers faced a sovereign's dilemma between a coherent elite and their own security `[wang2022book]`, shaped by elite kin geography `[wang2022]`. Size created agency problems `[sng2014]`. Maoist workplaces built clientelist authority `[walder1986]`. Great Leap radicalism followed career incentives `[kungchen2011]`. Cultural Revolution violence lowers trust today `[wang2019]`.

**Gap.** Historical political economy of China is growing fast, but links between imperial or Maoist legacies and contemporary behavior (beyond trust) remain few.

## 14. Hong Kong, Taiwan, Xinjiang and ethnic politics

Hong Kong localism rejects the Chinese nation as a basis of identity `[veg2017]`, `[kaeding2017]`, and the center and periphery produced competing nationalisms `[fong2017]`. Street politics diffused in a hybrid regime `[cheng2016]`. Xinjiang moved to preventive repression and surveillance `[greitens2020]`, `[leibold2019]`. Taiwan's military value `[greentalmadge2022]` and the case for a grand bargain `[glaser2015]` are contested, and territorial dispute behavior follows China's bargaining position `[fravel2008]`.

**Gap.** Post-2020 Hong Kong under the National Security Law and Taiwan's domestic politics under rising pressure are underrepresented in the top-cited literature, which favors older work.

## 15. China in the world: statecraft, multilateralism and order

**Claimed.** China was largely status quo in the 1990s `[johnston2003]` and internationalized through domestic channels `[zweig2002]`.

**Settled.** China's multilateralism is diverse and issue-specific `[wuthnow2012]`, `[kastner2016ss]`, `[johnston2019]`, shaped by domestic regime priorities `[weiss2021]`. Chinese finance is politically allocated and less conditional `[dreher2018]`, `[dreher2022]`, `[kaplan2016]`, and creates outside options `[qian2023]`. China coerces informally `[lim2022]`, `[kim2025refusals]`, but trade buys little influence `[kastner2016]`, and influence depends on intentionality, intermediaries and institutions `[fung2023]`, `[kastnerpearson2021]`.

**Contested.** Whether China props up autocracies: there is little systematic evidence `[bader2014]`, and China defends autocracy without exporting it `[weiss2019ssrn]`. Belt and Road motives are debated `[yu2016]`, `[zhouesteban2018]`. Weaker states hedge `[kuik2016]`. Industrial policy challenges US structural power `[malkin2020]`.

**Gap.** China's role in shaping international standards (technology, data, AI governance) and the domestic coalitions behind it are understudied relative to finance and trade.

## 16. Land, hukou and urban–rural politics

The hukou system was devolved, not abolished `[wingchan2008]`. Urban land became the base of local state power `[hsing2006]`, `[ong2014]`. Tax reform hollowed out township governments `[kennedy2007]`, `[smith2010]`. Decollectivization drove early agricultural growth `[lin1992]`.

**Gap.** Land finance is collapsing with the property downturn. Its effects on local government behavior, debt and central-local relations are a pressing, fast-moving research area.

## Cross-cutting roadmap: where your work fits

1. **China after 2015 needs re-testing.** Sections 1, 2, 3 and 5 rest on pre-2015 data. Recentralization, purges, the security turn and lost data access make "what changed under Xi" the field's central question. It also connects to your interest in autocratic adaptation.
2. **Text as the new data source.** Field and survey access has shrunk, while official text (speeches, documents, government portals, state media) has grown. Validated LLM annotation of official text is the most promising way to measure policy priorities, loyalty signals, ideological campaigns and responsiveness. That is your methods comparative advantage.
3. **Party-state capitalism as a variable.** Section 9's qualitative concept can be measured at the firm level and linked to foreign screening, sanctions exposure and dual-use trade. This bridges China studies to the IPE map's geoeconomics section.
4. **China and the autocratic order.** Section 15 shows limited evidence of deliberate autocracy promotion. Your work on autocratic rise and order contestation can sharpen the distinction between China making the world safe for autocracy and actively spreading it.
