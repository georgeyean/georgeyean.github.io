# South and Southeast Asian politics, c. 1964–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 350 work nodes, 18 theme nodes and 422 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[varshney2002]`) point to entries in `graph.json`. China has its own map and appears here only as a regional actor.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 47 works already in other maps are reused. Most come from comparative politics (clientelism, authoritarian institutions, ethnic identity, Slater and Malesky on Southeast Asia). Others come from REP (Chandra, Wilkinson, Horowitz), political economy (Evans, Olken, Besley and Burgess), security and IR (Kalyvas, Kocher, Goh, Acharya), the China map (Kuik, Malesky and London, Rithmire, Greitens) and American politics (Levitsky and Ziblatt).
- **Top-cited articles:** 155 come from the most-cited 2006–2026 articles in area journals (*Journal of East Asian Studies*, *Journal of Contemporary Asia*, *The Pacific Review*, *Contemporary Southeast Asia*, *Asian Survey*, *Pacific Affairs*, *India Review*, *Studies in Indian Politics*, *Journal of Southeast Asian Studies*) and region-specific articles in *Democratization*, *Journal of Democracy*, *World Politics*, *Comparative Politics*, SCID, APSR, AJPS, JOP, *International Security*, *QJPS* and economics journals.
- **Anchors:** 104 books and classic articles, from Kothari's Congress system and Scott's patron-client politics to Johnson, Amsden and Wade on developmental states, Sidel and Hutchcroft on the Philippines, Robison and Hadiz on Indonesian oligarchy, and Acharya on ASEAN.

Relations are judgments of how each work's argument relates to the other's, and every relation runs from the later work to the earlier one. One-line claims for works not in the library are my summaries.

## 1. Indian democracy and the party system
**Claimed.** Congress ruled as a dominant-party 'system' `[kothari1964]` built through patronage and adaptation `[weiner1967]`; consociational features explain India's survival `[lijphart1996]`. **Settled.** Congress decayed into a crisis of governability `[kohli1990]`, giving way to a fragmented third electoral system of regional and lower-caste parties `[yadav1999]`, `[chhibber1999]`. Party-system fragmentation tracks the centralization of power `[chhibberkollman2004]`, `[hicken2009]`. **Contested.** Whether the BJP has built a second dominant-party system `[chhibberverma2019]` or rests on a fragmented opposition `[ziegfeld2020]`; whether regional parties reflect identity or clientelist organization `[ziegfeld2016]`, `[ziegfeld2012]`. Democratic origins: founding party strength `[tudor2013book]` versus class structure `[moore1966]`. **Gaps.** How the end of Congress dominance and the BJP's rise fit one theory of party organization `[tudorziegfeld2019]`.

## 2. Hindu nationalism and the BJP
**Claimed.** Hindu nationalism grew through ethnoreligious mobilization and strategic moderation `[jaffrelot1996]` and middle-class anxieties `[hansen1999]`. **Settled.** The BJP reaches poor voters through service-providing affiliates `[thachil2011]`, `[thachil2014]`, and welfare plus nationalism drew lower-caste voters in 2019 `[jaffrelot2019sip]`. **Contested.** Whether it is best read as populist `[mcdonnellcabrera2018]`, `[varshney2021scid]` or as an ethnic democracy project `[jaffrelot2021]`; whether coalitions moderated it `[ruparelia2006]`. **Gaps.** Whether a consolidated Hindu vote has replaced caste head counts `[sircar2022]`, and what centralization means for federalism `[aiyartillin2020]`.

## 3. Caste, ethnicity, quotas and identity politics
**Claimed.** Ethnic parties outbid and destabilize democracy `[horowitz1985]`. **Settled.** In patronage democracies, ethnic parties win where head counts and patronage align `[chandra2004]`, and ethnic identities are multiple and constructed `[chandra2006]`, `[chandrawilkinson2008]`. Mandated representation shifts policy `[pande2003]`, `[chattopadhyayduflo2004]` and has lasting effects `[bhavnani2009]`. **Contested.** Whether caste quotas in village councils change distribution `[dunningnilekani2013]` or mainly perceptions `[chauchard2017]`; who gains from reservations `[jaffrelot2006]`, `[bertrand2010]`. **Gaps.** Ethnic politics in Southeast Asian democratization `[aspinall2011]`, `[pepinsky2009]`.

## 4. Communal and ethnic riots
**Claimed.** Interethnic civic ties prevent Hindu-Muslim riots `[varshney2002]`. **Settled.** Riots are politically produced `[brass2003]` and state governments stop them when minority votes matter `[wilkinson2004]`, `[nellis2016]`. **Contested.** Economic drivers: growth reduces riots `[bohlkensergenti2010]` but rising Muslim incomes may raise them `[mitraray2014]`; deep historical roots `[jha2013]`, `[jhawilkinson2012]`. **Gaps.** Cross-regional comparison with Indonesia's communal wars `[vanklinken2007]`, `[tajima2014]`.

## 5. Clientelism, brokers and vote buying
**Claimed.** Patron-client ties structure Southeast Asian politics `[scott1972]`. **Settled.** Clientelism is a distinct linkage `[kitschelt2007]`, `[hicken2011]` with machine monitoring `[stokes2005]`; Indonesian clientelism is freewheeling and runs through personal success teams `[aspinallberenschot2019]`, `[berenschot2018]`. **Contested.** Whether brokers are agents of parties or chosen by clients `[auerbachthachil2018]`; whether handouts buy votes or signal viability `[chauchard2018]`, `[aspinall2017jeas]`; whether citizens bypass brokers `[bussell2019]`. **Gaps.** Clientelism beyond parties `[aspinallhicken2019]`; criminal candidates `[vaishnav2017]`.

## 6. Federalism, decentralization and local governance
**Claimed.** Decentralization can preserve markets `[weingast1995]` but risks elite capture `[bardhan2002]`. **Settled.** India's state-nation federalism accommodates diversity `[stepan2011]`, `[adeney2017]`; regional elites shape development `[sinha2005]`. **Contested.** Indonesia's big-bang decentralization had small economic effects `[pepinskywihardja2011]` and invited district proliferation `[pierskalla2016]`; recentralization improved services in Vietnam `[maleskynguyentran2014]`. **Gaps.** Citizen claim-making and local democracy `[kruks2018]`, `[parthasarathy2019]`.

## 7. Military rule and civil-military relations
**Claimed.** Praetorianism arises from weak institutions `[huntington1968]`; Pakistan's security fears entrenched the army `[jalal1990]`. **Settled.** India and Pakistan diverged on legitimacy, institutions and coup-proofing `[staniland2008]`, `[wilkinson2015]`; Pakistan's army acts on guardian beliefs `[shah2014]` and economic stakes `[siddiqa2007]`. **Contested.** Founding-party versus security explanations `[tudor2013book]`. **Gaps.** Military tutelage inside hybrid regimes `[adeney2015]`; Bangladesh `[riaz2020]`; Indonesia's withdrawal `[mietzner2009]`.

## 8. Insurgency and civil conflict
**Claimed.** Peasants rebel against violations of subsistence norms `[scott1976]`. **Settled.** Violence follows control `[kalyvas2006]`, `[kocher2011]`; prewar networks shape insurgent cohesion `[staniland2012]`, `[staniland2014]`; Sri Lanka's war came from majoritarian outbidding `[tambiah1986]`, `[devotta2004]`. **Contested.** Unemployment and violence `[berman2011]` versus program effects `[dasgupta2017]`; poverty and support for militancy in Pakistan `[shapirofair2010]`, `[blair2012]`, `[fair2012]`. **Gaps.** Postwar accountability `[croninfurman2019]`; the social legacies of war in Nepal `[gilligan2013]`, `[dejuanpierskalla2014]`.

## 9. Developmental states and the political economy of growth
**Claimed.** Insulated bureaucracies guided East Asian growth `[johnson1982]`, `[amsden1989]`, `[wade1990]`. **Settled.** Embedded, cohesive states industrialize `[evans1995]`, `[kohli2004]`, and systemic vulnerability explains where they arise `[doner2005]`. **Contested.** Whether Southeast Asia fits the model `[doner2009]`, `[kuhonta2011]`; whether corruption blocks development `[kang2002]`. **Gaps.** India's pro-business turn `[kohli2007scid]`; colonial legacies `[banerjeeiyer2005]`, `[iyer2010]`.

## 10. Authoritarian durability
**Claimed.** Ruling parties make autocracies durable `[brownlee2007book]`, `[geddes1999]`. **Settled.** Protection pacts formed under contentious politics built strong states and parties in Malaysia and Singapore `[slater2010]`; coalitions decided who survived the Asian crisis `[pepinsky2009book]`. **Contested.** Ordering power versus party institutions `[brownlee2008jeas]`; Cambodia as personalist rather than party rule `[morgenbesser2017]`. **Gaps.** Single-party responsiveness in Vietnam `[malesky2011cpol]`, `[maleskyschuler2020]`; strong-state democratization `[slater2012jod]`.

## 11. Indonesian democratization and oligarchy
**Claimed.** Oligarchy survived democratization `[robisonhadiz2004]`, `[winters2011]`. **Settled.** Democracy survived by accommodating elites `[aspinall2010]`, with party cartels limiting accountability `[slater2004]`, `[slater2018]`. **Contested.** Elite versus mass drivers of democratic regression `[warburton2018]`, `[aspinall2019demz]`, `[mietzner2019]`. **Gaps.** Long-run effects of decentralization `[hadiz2010]`.

## 12. The Philippines: bossism, dynasties and populism
**Claimed.** Cacique democracy run by landed families `[anderson1988]`, `[mccoy1993]`. **Settled.** Bossism through state resources and coercion `[sidel1999]`; booty capitalism `[hutchcroft1998]`; incumbency entrenches dynasties `[querubin2016]`. **Contested.** What drives Duterte's support: grievances `[curato2016]`, penal populism `[kennyholmes2020]` or ethnoregional identity `[dulay2022]`. **Gaps.** Weak resistance to aggrandizement `[thompson2020demz]`.

## 13. Thailand: monarchy, coups and polarization
**Claimed.** A bureaucratic polity `[riggs1966]`. **Settled.** A palace-centered network monarchy `[mccargo2005]`, `[handley2006]` clashed with Thaksin's electoral populism `[phongpaichitbaker2004]`, `[phongpaichitbaker2008]`, enabled by the 1997 constitution `[hicken2006]`, `[kuhonta2008]`. **Contested.** The role of courts `[dressel2010]`, `[merieau2016]`. **Gaps.** Junta-managed elections `[ricks2019]`.

## 14. Myanmar's military, ethnic politics and transition
**Settled.** War against insurgents made the army the state `[callahan2003]`, `[smith1991]`; ceasefire capitalism extended its reach `[woods2011]`. **Contested.** Whether the transition was driven by political economy `[jones2013jca]`, peripheral pacification `[jones2014demz]` or military design `[callahan2012]`. **Gaps.** Exclusion of the Rohingya `[cheesman2017]`; strategic violence `[christensen2019]`.

## 15. Religion, Islam and politics
**Claimed.** Civil Islam supports democracy `[hefner2000]`. **Settled.** Rulers use Islamization to build power `[nasr2001]`, `[liow2009]`; secular politicians adopt sharia bylaws `[buehler2013]`, `[buehler2016]`. **Contested.** Tolerance without liberalism `[menchik2016]`, `[mietzner2020]`; Islamic populism `[hadiz2018]`. **Gaps.** Religion and voting `[sumaktoyo2021]`.

## 16. Corruption, bureaucracy and accountability
**Settled.** Corruption is a system `[wade1982]`, measurable through field experiments `[olken2007]`, `[bertrand2007]`. **Contested.** Media and voter information `[besleyburgess2002]`, `[pande2011]`; predictable versus unpredictable corruption `[maleskysamphantharak2008]`. **Gaps.** Bureaucratic capacity in India `[gulzarpasquale2016]`, `[dasguptakapur2020]`.

## 17. Populism and democratic backsliding
**Settled.** Elected leaders erode democracy from within `[levitskyziblatt2018]`, `[haggardkaufman2021]`, as in India `[varshney2022]`, `[tudor2023]`. **Contested.** Voter demand for order `[pepinsky2017jod]` versus elite-led innovation `[curatofossati2020]`. **Gaps.** Comparing South and Southeast Asian cases.

## 18. ASEAN, regionalism and great-power competition
**Claimed.** ASEAN manages a balance of power `[leifer1989]`. **Settled.** It built norm-based community `[acharya2001]`, `[acharya2004]`, and Southeast Asian states hedge `[kuik2008]`, `[goh2008]`. **Contested.** Process versus progress `[jonessmith2007]`; non-interference in practice `[jones2010pr]`. **Gaps.** Public opinion on hedging `[fangli2022]`.
