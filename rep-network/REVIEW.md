# Race, ethnicity and politics, c. 1903–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 360 work nodes, 20 theme nodes and 393 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[dawson1994]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 88 works already in the American politics, political theory, comparative politics, IPE, security and IR maps are reused, including the race theme of the American politics map (Kinder & Sanders, Mendelberg, Tesler, Jardina, Hajnal & Lee) and the race theme of the political theory map (Mills, Shelby, Crenshaw, Collins).
- **Library seeds:** four works from your `papers.json`: Horowitz, Rosenberg, Bratsberg et al. and Búzás 2013.
- **Top-cited articles:** 161 come from the 400 most-cited 2006–2026 articles in REP journals (*Journal of Race, Ethnicity, and Politics*, *Politics, Groups, and Identities*, *Du Bois Review*, *Ethnic and Racial Studies*, *Nationalism and Ethnic Politics*, *JEMS*, *International Migration Review*) and race-related articles in APSR, AJPS, JOP, BJPS, *Political Behavior*, POQ, PRQ, APR, *Political Psychology*, *Perspectives on Politics*, ARPS, LSQ, SAPD, QJPS and CPS.
- **Anchors:** 107 books and classic articles, from Du Bois, Myrdal, Key and Allport to Dawson, Mendelberg, Masuoka & Junn, Fraga and Vitalis.

Every anchor and library seed was checked in Crossref. 87 matched directly; 24 books exist in Crossref only through reviews of them. One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries, not re-read this session.

## 1. Race, the state and American political development
**Claimed.** The color line is the problem of the century `[dubois1903]`; racial inequality contradicts the American creed `[myrdal1944]`. **Settled.** The one-party South was built by local racial threat `[key1949]` and deliberate disfranchisement `[kousser1974]`. American politics carries ascriptive traditions `[smith1993]` and competing racial orders `[kingsmith2005]`. New Deal policy widened racial gaps `[katznelson2005]`. **Contested.** How durable progress is `[klinkner1999]`, `[valelly2004]`; slavery's legacy in today's attitudes `[acharya2016]`. **Gaps.** Racial orders after Obama and Trump `[smithking2021]`.

## 2. Racial attitudes
**Claimed.** Prejudice is a sense of group position `[blumer1958]`, or symbolic racism `[kindersears1981]`. **Settled.** Racial resentment predicts white policy opinion `[kindersanders1996]`, now joined by general ethnocentrism `[kinderkam2009]`. **Contested.** Principle versus prejudice `[sniderman1993]`; what the resentment scale measures `[kamburge2018]`, `[huddyfeldman2009]`; whether partisanship now drives racial attitudes `[engelhardt2020]`. **Gaps.** Racial sympathy `[chudy2021]` and dehumanization `[jardinapiston2023]` are new, undermeasured dimensions.

## 3. Racial priming and appeals
**Settled.** Campaign cues prime racial attitudes `[mendelberg2001]`, `[valentino2002]`. **Contested.** Implicit versus explicit appeals `[huberlapinski2006]`, `[mendelberg2008]`; whether the norm of equality has collapsed `[valentino2018]`, `[newman2020]`. **Gaps.** How appeals work in a two-party world where Democrats also distance `[stephensdougan2020]`.

## 4. White identity and backlash
**Settled.** Demographic threat moves whites rightward `[craigricheson2014]`; white identity is distinct from resentment `[jardina2019]`. Racism and sexism explain the 2016 white education gap `[schaffner2018]`. **Contested.** Status threat versus economic hardship `[mutz2018]`, `[baccini2021]`. **Gaps.** White identity and support for democracy `[bartels2020]`, `[millerdavis2020]`.

## 5. Group consciousness and linked fate
**Settled.** Linked fate as a group-interest heuristic `[dawson1994]`, extended to Latinos `[sanchez2006]` and Asian Americans `[junnmasuoka2008]`. **Contested.** The measure `[gay2016]`; limits from respectability politics `[bunyasi2019]`; a shared people-of-color identity `[searssavalei2006]`, `[perez2020]`. **Gaps.** Social enforcement of group norms `[whitelaird2020]`.

## 6. Black politics and protest
**Settled.** The civil rights movement grew from indigenous organizations `[morris1984]` and political opportunity `[mcadam1982]`; protest moves elites and opinion `[gillion2013]`, `[wasow2020]`. **Contested.** Violent versus nonviolent protest `[wasow2020]`, `[enos2019]`; secondary marginalization `[cohen1999]`. **Gaps.** BLM's long-run effects `[renynewman2021]`, `[mazumder2018]`.

## 7. Latino politics
**Settled.** Ethnic cues mobilize `[barreto2010]`; threat mobilizes `[perez2014]`, `[white2016]`. **Contested.** Whether "Latino" is a unified political identity `[beltran2010]`. **Gaps.** The Latino rightward shift after 2016.

## 8. Asian American politics
**Settled.** Panethnicity was state-made `[espiritu1992]`; Asian Americans are racially triangulated `[kim1999]`. Partisanship follows social exclusion `[kuo2017]`. **Gaps.** Anti-Asian hostility after COVID-19 `[renybarreto2020]`.

## 9. Immigration attitudes and enforcement
**Settled.** Sociotropic and cultural concerns outweigh personal labor-market interest `[sidescitrin2007]`, `[hainmueller2010]`, `[hainmuellerhopkins2015]`. **Contested.** Labor competition for some workers `[malhotra2013]`; local context `[hopkins2010]`. **Gaps.** Enforcement's effects on immigrants themselves `[maltby2020]`, `[bratsberg2021]`.

## 10. Representation
**Claimed.** Descriptive representation is not needed for substantive representation `[swain1993]`. **Settled.** Black legislators do advance Black interests `[broockman2013]`, `[grose2011]`; legislators discriminate against Black constituents `[butlerbroockman2011]`. **Contested.** When descriptive representation matters `[mansbridge1999]`, `[griffin2014]`. **Gaps.** Candidate supply `[juenkeshah2016]`.

## 11. Voting rights and election administration
**Settled.** VRA districting raised minority officeholding `[grofman1992]`, with trade-offs `[lublin1997]`, `[cameron1996]`. **Contested.** Voter ID effects `[hajnal2017]` against null findings `[cantonipons2021]`. **Gaps.** Post-*Shelby* evidence `[komisarchikwhite2025]`.

## 12. Participation and turnout
**Settled.** Resources and recruitment `[verba1995]`; empowerment `[bobogilliam1990]`. **Contested.** Whether coethnic candidates or district context close turnout gaps `[gay2001]`, `[fraga2018]`. **Gaps.** Policy feedback through Medicaid and policing `[michener2018]`.

## 13. Race and partisanship
**Settled.** Race realigned the parties `[carmines1989]`, and racial attitudes explain southern white defection `[kuziemkowashington2018]`. **Contested.** Timing: top-down 1964 versus a bottom-up 1930s–50s realignment `[schickler2016]`, `[feinsteinschickler2008]`. **Gaps.** Race and partisanship now inseparable `[westwoodpeterson2020]`.

## 14. Policing and the carceral state
**Settled.** Mass incarceration is concentrated by race and class `[western2006]` and depresses citizenship `[lermanweaver2014]`, `[burch2013]`. **Contested.** Elite frontlash `[weaver2007]` versus public punitiveness `[enns2014]` and Black constituencies `[fortner2013]`; liberal origins `[murakawa2014]`. **Gaps.** Bias hidden in administrative data `[knox2020]`.

## 15. Race and welfare policy
**Settled.** Racial exclusion shaped welfare `[quadagno1994]`, `[lieberman1998]`; racial stereotypes drive opposition `[gilens1999]`. **Gaps.** Racialization of new programs `[tesler2012]`.

## 16. Context, contact and segregation
**Settled.** Segregation is made by policy `[masseydenton1993]`, `[trounstine2018]`. **Contested.** Threat versus contact `[key1949]`, `[allport1954]`, `[enos2014]`. **Gaps.** Which interventions durably reduce prejudice `[kallabroockman2021]`.

## 17. Intersectionality
**Settled.** Single-axis analysis erases Black women `[crenshaw1989]`; intersectionality is a research paradigm `[hancock2007]`. **Gaps.** Quantitative operationalization `[gershon2019]`.

## 18. Race in political theory
**Settled.** Liberalism has a racial contract `[mills1997]`; color-blind ideology `[bonillasilva2003]`. **Contested.** Integration as an ideal `[anderson2010]` against solidarity-based views `[shelby2005]`. **Gaps.** Reparations theory.

## 19. Comparative ethnic politics
**Settled.** Ethnicity is constructed and activated by institutions `[brubaker2004]`, `[posner2005]`, `[chandra2004]`. **Contested.** Consociational `[lijphart1977]` versus centripetal design `[horowitz1985]`. **Gaps.** US–comparative dialogue `[segurarodrigues2006]`.

## 20. Race in international politics
**Settled.** IR was founded as race relations `[vitalis2015]`; settler states built a global color line `[lakereynolds2008]`. **Gaps.** Measuring race in IR outcomes `[freeman2022]`, `[rosenberg2019]`, `[greenriley2023]`.
