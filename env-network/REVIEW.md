# Environmental politics, c. 1968–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 252 work nodes, 17 theme nodes and 251 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[ostrom1990]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Your library:** six works come from `papers.json`. They are Arias & Blair on hurricane migration, Cao & Prakash on trade and regulation, Roger, Hale & Andonova on transnational governance, Bilal & Rossi-Hansberg, Barrett's *Environment and Statecraft* and Green's *Rethinking Private Authority*.
- **Shared works:** 27 works already in the IR, IPE, security, comparative, China and political theory maps are reused. Examples are Keohane & Victor, Colgan, Green & Hale, Ross on oil and Caney on climate justice.
- **Top-cited articles:** 167 come from the 400 most-cited 2006–2026 articles in nine environmental journals plus political science journals. The environmental journals are *Global Environmental Politics*, *Environmental Politics*, *Global Environmental Change*, *Nature Climate Change*, *Climate Policy*, the *Annual Review of Environment and Resources*, *Earth System Governance*, *WIREs Climate Change* and *Energy Research & Social Science*. For the science-heavy journals, only politics, governance and policy articles were kept.
- **Anchors:** 52 books and classic articles, from Hardin and Ostrom to Stokes, Mildenberger and Aklin & Urpelainen.

One-line claims for non-library works are my summaries, not re-read this session.

## 1. International climate regime and negotiations
**Claimed.** Global commons need self-enforcing treaties `[barrett2003]`. Universal UN talks produce gridlock `[victor2011]`. **Settled.** The regime is a fragmented complex `[keohanevictor2011]`, and Paris shifted to bottom-up pledges `[falkner2016]`, `[hale2016]`. **Contested.** Free riding versus domestic distributive conflict as the core barrier `[aklinmildenberger2020]`. Clubs `[nordhaus2015]` versus inclusive minilateralism `[eckersley2012]`. **Gaps.** Evidence on whether pledge-and-review ratchets ambition remains thin.

## 2. Transnational and private governance
**Claimed.** Market-driven private authority can govern `[cashore2002]`. **Settled.** Transnational and city networks are pervasive `[andonova2009]`, `[bulkeley2014]`, and IOs orchestrate them `[haleroger2014]`. **Contested.** Accountability and legitimacy `[backstrand2008]`, `[backstrandkuyper2017]` and the effects of disclosure `[dingwerth2010]`. **Gaps.** Additionality of nonstate pledges is rarely measured.

## 3. Governance architecture and earth system governance
**Claimed.** Planetary boundaries `[rockstrom2009]` require earth system governance `[biermann2007]`. **Settled.** Fragmentation has synergistic and conflictive forms `[biermann2009]`. **Contested.** Polycentric versus centralized designs `[ostrom2010]`, `[dorschflachsland2017]`. **Gaps.** Linking SDG governance to outcomes `[glassnewig2019]`.

## 4. Political economy of climate policy
**Claimed.** Domestic institutions and interests explain climate policy `[harrisonsundstrom2010]`. **Settled.** Business coalitions shape instruments `[meckling2011]`, and green industrial policy builds supportive coalitions `[meckling2015]`, `[allan2021]`. **Contested.** Asset revaluation `[colgan2021]` versus carbon interests' double representation `[mildenberger2020]`. Utility retrenchment `[stokes2020]`. **Gaps.** Compensation designs that secure durable support `[gaikwad2022]`.

## 5. Public opinion and climate attitudes
**Claimed.** Concern rises with wealth and experience. **Settled.** U.S. opinion is partisan `[eganmullin2017]`, and literacy polarizes `[kahan2012]`. **Contested.** Whether local exposure moves opinion `[bergquistwarshaw2019]`, `[hazlettmildenberger2020]`, `[ariasblair2024]`. **Gaps.** Global South opinion is understudied.

## 6. Denial, skepticism and the countermovement
**Settled.** An organized countermovement manufactured doubt `[oreskesconway2010]`, `[jacques2008]`, `[brulle2014]`, and companies knew `[supranoreskes2017]`. **Contested.** Elite cues `[merkleystecula2021]` versus identity `[mccrightdunlap2011]`. **Gaps.** Denial is shifting to delay, which is harder to measure.

## 7. Parties, populism and electoral politics
**Settled.** The populist right links to skepticism `[lockwood2018]`, `[huber2020]`, `[kulin2021]`. **Contested.** Electoral backlash to climate policy `[stokes2016]`, `[patterson2023]`. **Gaps.** Green parties in government.

## 8. Policy instruments
**Settled.** Carbon pricing works when revenues are recycled visibly `[carattini2018]`, `[klenert2018]`, `[rabe2018]`. **Contested.** Pricing versus sequencing industrial policy `[meckling2015]`. **Gaps.** Central banks and finance as climate instruments `[campiglio2018]`.

## 9. Energy transitions
**Settled.** Carbon lock-in `[unruh2000]`, `[seto2016]`. Transitions are multi-level `[geels2002]`. **Contested.** Speed `[sovacool2016]` and additions versus replacement `[yorkbell2019]`. **Gaps.** Geopolitics of critical minerals `[riofrancos2023]`, `[overland2019]`.

## 10. Environmental and climate justice
**Settled.** Injustice is distributional, procedural and recognitional `[schlosberg2007]`, `[jenkinsenergy2016]`. **Contested.** Just transition designs `[wanglo2021]`, `[sovacool2021]`. **Gaps.** Multispecies and Indigenous justice `[celermajer2021envp]`, `[whyte2020]`.

## 11. Adaptation, vulnerability and resilience
**Settled.** Adaptation is political `[eriksen2015]` and can be maladaptive `[barnettoneill2010]`. **Contested.** Resilience versus transformation `[pelling2011]`. **Gaps.** Political scientists neglect adaptation `[javeline2014]`.

## 12. Commons and natural resource governance
**Settled.** Communities can govern commons `[ostrom1990]` against Hardin `[hardin1968]`. **Contested.** Power in polycentric systems `[morrison2019]`. **Gaps.** Scaling commons insights to global problems.

## 13. Natural resources and the resource curse
**Settled.** Oil sustains autocracy under some conditions `[ross2001]`, `[ross2012]`. **Contested.** Long-run evidence `[habermenaldo2011]` and conditional effects `[luongweinthal2006]`. **Gaps.** A green resource curse in transition minerals.

## 14. Climate, conflict and migration
**Claimed.** Scarcity causes conflict `[homerdixon1999]`. **Contested.** Temperature and conflict `[burke2009]`, `[hsiang2013]` versus skeptics `[buhaug2010]`. Syria `[kelley2015]`. **Gaps.** Migration responses as adaptation `[mclemanhunter2010]`.

## 15. The environmental state, democracy and authoritarianism
**Contested.** Whether democracies outperform `[lireuveny2006]`, `[povitkina2018]` or authoritarian environmentalism works `[gilley2012]`, `[eatonkostka2014]`. **Gaps.** The environmental state's glass ceiling `[hausknost2020]`.

## 16. Trade, finance and corporate power
**Contested.** Race to the bottom `[konisky2007]` versus trading up `[vogel1995]`. The visibility of regulation matters `[caoprakash2012]`. **Gaps.** Green macrofinance `[gaborbraun2025]` connects to your IPE work.

## 17. Movements, activism and litigation
**Settled.** Climate movements split tactically `[hadden2015]`. **Gaps.** Litigation's policy effects `[setzervanhala2019]`.
