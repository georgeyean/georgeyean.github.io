# European politics, c. 1950–2026: where it went, what settled, what is open

A narrative companion to `graph.json` and `network.html` in this folder. The graph has 355 work nodes, 19 theme nodes and 426 typed relations. Each theme section follows the same order: **what was claimed → what the evidence settled → what is contested → gaps and research opportunities**. Node ids in brackets (e.g. `[haas1958]`) point to entries in `graph.json`.

**How the works were chosen.**
- **Shared works:** 50 works already in other maps are reused. Most (24) come from the comparative politics map: populism and the radical right, backsliding, welfare states and varieties of capitalism, and new cleavages. Others come from political economy and IPE (the eurozone crisis, Brexit and trade shocks, social Europe), IR (liberal theory, the Court of Justice, EU accession), race and ethnic politics and American politics (immigration attitudes, cultural backlash), technology (the Brussels effect, automation and the populist right), political psychology (Brexit identities), environment (EU climate diplomacy) and political theory (the EU's legitimacy deficits).
- **Top-cited articles:** 166 come from the most-cited 2006–2026 articles in the *Journal of European Public Policy* (41), *JCMS* (31), *West European Politics* (13), *European Union Politics* (12), *Party Politics*, *Government and Opposition*, the *Journal of European Integration*, *Comparative European Politics*, the *Journal of European Social Policy*, *Electoral Studies* and *EJPR*. European articles in *Comparative Political Studies* (12), ARPS, IO, APSR, AJPS, BJPS, JOP, *World Politics*, *Journal of Democracy*, *Perspectives on Politics*, *Socio-Economic Review*, QJE and JEMS were added too.
- **Anchors:** 95 books and classic articles, from Haas, Hoffmann and Lipset & Rokkan through Moravcsik, Scharpf, Majone, Manners, Mudde and Kriesi et al. to Brexit and backsliding studies.

Relations are judgments of how each work's argument relates to the other's, and every relation runs from the later work to the earlier one. One-line claims are my summaries.

## 1. Integration theory
**Claimed.** Sectoral spillover and supranational loyalty transfer drive integration `[haas1958]`; states resist in high politics `[hoffmann1966]` and integration rescued rather than replaced them `[milward1992]`. **Settled.** Grand bargains follow national economic preferences and bargaining power `[moravcsik1993]`, `[moravcsik1998]`, while path dependence `[pierson1996path]`, supranational governance `[sandholtzstonesweet1998]` and multilevel governance `[marks1996]`, `[hooghemarks2001]` capture what happens between treaties. **Contested.** Whether mass politicization now constrains integration `[hooghemarks2009]` or the euro crisis shows the opposite `[schimmelfennig2014jei]`; whether a "new intergovernmentalism" exists `[bickerton2014]`, `[schimmelfennig2015jcms]`; failing forward as a synthesis `[jones2016]`, `[jones2021]`. **Gaps.** Why the euro and Schengen crises diverged `[schimmelfennig2018]`, `[borzelrisse2018]`, `[genschel2018]`, and whether the EU can build fiscal and military capacity `[kelemenmcnamara2022]`.

## 2. EU institutions and legislative politics
**Claimed.** Decision rules decide whether the EU looks intergovernmental or supranational `[tsebelis1994]`, `[tsebelisgarrett2001]`; delegation gives agents bounded autonomy `[pollack1997]`. **Settled.** The European Parliament votes mainly along left-right party lines `[hix2006]`, `[hix2007]` and has steadily gained power `[hixhoyland2013]`; the Council works by consensus `[hayesrenshaw2006]`. **Contested.** Sources of bargaining power in the European Council `[tallberg2008]`; whether the Commission lost or gained from the crisis `[bauerbecker2014]`, `[bickerton2014]`. **Gaps.** Responsiveness of Council governments to domestic opinion `[hagemann2016]`.

## 3. European courts and legal integration
**Claimed.** The Court of Justice constitutionalized the treaties `[weiler1991]` through a neofunctionalist logic `[burleymattli1993]`. **Settled.** Litigation and national judges' incentives drove supremacy `[stonesweetbrunell1998]`, `[alter2001]`, `[stonesweet2004]`. **Contested.** How far the Court bends to governments `[garrett1995]`, `[carrubba2008]`, `[larssonnaurin2016]`; whether it disembeds `[hoepnerschaefer2012]` or re-embeds markets `[caporaso2009]`. **Gaps.** Courts as a defence against national backsliding `[blaubergerkelemen2017]`.

## 4. Europeanization and compliance
**Claimed.** Misfit plus mediating factors drives domestic change `[borzelrisse2000]`, `[cowles2001]`. **Settled.** Domestic politics and capacity matter more than fit `[mastenbroek2006]`, `[borzel2010]`. **Contested.** Worlds of compliance `[falkner2007]`, `[falknertreib2008]` versus good post-accession records `[sedelmeier2008]`. **Gaps.** Europeanization as diffusion `[borzelrisse2012]`.

## 5. Democratic deficit and legitimacy
**Claimed.** Input legitimacy is weak and output legitimacy limited `[scharpf1999]`. **Contested.** No deficit for a regulatory polity `[majone1998]`, `[moravcsik2002]` versus a deficit of contestation `[follesdalhix2006]`, `[mair2007]`; demoicracy as an alternative standard `[nicolaidis2012]`. **Settled.** Crisis governance weakened democratic and throughput legitimacy `[crum2013]`, `[schmidt2013]`, `[sanchezcuenca2017]`. **Gaps.** Technocracy and populism as twin threats to party democracy `[caramani2017]`, `[mair2013]`.

## 6. Public opinion, Euroscepticism and identity
**Claimed.** Support follows utilitarian gains `[gabel1998]`. **Settled.** Identity and cues matter as much as calculation `[mclaren2002]`, `[hooghemarks2005]`, `[hoboltdevries2016]`; attitudes are multidimensional `[boomgaarden2011]`; the education gap is widening `[hakhverdian2013]`. **Contested.** Whether crisis Euroscepticism was economic or identity-driven `[serricchio2013]`, `[kuhnstoeckel2014]`, `[hoboltwratil2015]`. **Gaps.** Left versus right Euroscepticism `[vanelsas2016]`; benchmarking the EU against national systems `[devries2018]`.

## 7. Politicization
**Claimed.** A latent EU dimension awaits mobilization `[vandereijkfranklin2004]`. **Settled.** Politicization grew with EU authority `[dewilde2011]`, `[dewildezurn2012]`, largely through national campaigns led by the radical right `[huttergrande2014]`, `[hutter2016]`. **Contested.** Whether mainstream parties can keep the giant asleep `[greenpedersen2012]` or challengers wake it `[devrieshobolt2012]`, `[hoboltdevries2015]`. **Gaps.** Politicization beyond the EU `[zurn2019]`, `[devries2021]`.

## 8. European Parliament elections and referendums
**Settled.** EP elections are second-order `[reifschmitt1980]`, `[vandereijkfranklin1996]`, `[hixmarsh2007]`. **Contested.** How much EU attitudes now matter `[hobolt2009bjps]`, `[hoboltspoon2012]`, `[hoboltdevries2016elst]`; referendums as votes on Europe `[hobolt2009europe]`. **Gaps.** Personalization through lead candidates `[hobolt2014jepp]`.

## 9. EMU and the eurozone crisis
**Claimed.** Europe was not an optimum currency area `[mundell1961]`; ideas made EMU possible `[mcnamara1998]`. **Settled.** The crisis was a balance-of-payments crisis set in mismatched growth models `[copelovitch2016]`, `[hall2014]`, `[frieden2017]`, without a lender of last resort `[degrauwe2013]`. **Contested.** Austerity's rationale `[blyth2013]`, `[matthijsmcnamara2015]`; whether reform was intergovernmental `[schimmelfennig2015li]` or neofunctional `[niemannioannou2015]`. **Gaps.** Electoral fallout `[hernandezkriesi2016]`, `[hobolttilley2016]` and legitimacy costs `[armingeon2015]`.

## 10. Enlargement and conditionality
**Claimed.** Liberal norms trapped reluctant members into enlarging `[schimmelfennig2001]`. **Settled.** Credible membership incentives drive rule adoption `[schimmelfennigsedelmeier2004]`, `[schimmelfennigsedelmeier2005]`, `[vachudova2005]`. **Contested.** Effects without a membership perspective `[kelley2006]`, `[schimmelfennigscholtz2008]` and in contested states `[noutcheva2009]`. **Gaps.** Conditionality and state capture `[richter2019]`, `[innes2014]`.

## 11. Democratic backsliding and the rule of law
**Settled.** Hungary and Poland followed a legalistic playbook `[bankuti2012]`, `[scheppele2018]`, `[pirrostanley2022]`. **Contested.** Whether the EU's tools can work `[sedelmeier2014]`, `[sedelmeier2017]`, `[blaubergerkelemen2017]` or the EU is locked in an authoritarian equilibrium `[kelemen2017]`, `[kelemen2020]`; whether backsliding is the right paradigm `[cianettihanley2021]`. **Gaps.** Political economy of dependent capitalism and illiberalism `[scheiring2022]`.

## 12. Cleavages, party systems and realignment
**Claimed.** Frozen cleavages `[lipsetrokkan1967]` versus value change `[inglehart1977]`. **Settled.** A new cultural cleavage around globalization `[kriesi2006]`, `[kriesi2008]`, `[bornschier2010]`, now transnational `[hoogemarks2018]`, with educated voters moving left `[gethin2022]`. **Contested.** Class realignment `[oeschrennwald2018]`, `[gingrichhausermann2015]`; mainstream convergence and decline `[spoonkluver2019]`. **Gaps.** Affective polarization in multiparty systems `[reiljan2020]`, `[hobolt2020]`.

## 13. Populism and the radical right
**Claimed.** A winning formula `[kitschelt1995]`; nativism at the core `[mudde2007]`. **Settled.** Immigration grievances unite the family `[ivarsflaten2008]`, `[rydgren2008]`; mainstream strategy shapes success `[meguid2005]`, `[abouchadikrause2020]`. **Contested.** Economic versus cultural causes `[colantone2018]`, `[norris2019]`, `[gidronhall2017]`, `[kurer2020]`. **Gaps.** Growth regimes and populism `[hopkinblyth2019]`; social integration `[gidronhall2020]`.

## 14. Welfare states and social Europe
**Claimed.** Market integration outran social integration `[leibfriedpierson1995]`, `[scharpf2010]`. **Settled.** Welfare boundaries opened `[ferrera2005]`; reforms dualized `[emmenegger2012]`. **Contested.** Social investment as remedy `[hemerijck2018]`; a socializing Semester `[zeitlinvanhercke2018]`. **Gaps.** Welfare chauvinism `[vanoorschot2006]`, `[schumacher2016]`.

## 15. Migration and asylum
**Settled.** Cultural concerns dominate attitudes `[sidescitrin2007]`, `[hainmuellerhiscox2007]`, `[hainmueller2014]`. **Contested.** Effects of refugee exposure `[hangartner2019]`, `[dinas2019]`, `[dustmann2019]`; convergence of integration policies `[joppke2007]`, `[koopmans2010]`. **Gaps.** Why EU asylum governance fails `[thielemann2018]`, `[zaun2018]`, `[lavenex2018]`.

## 16. Brexit
**Settled.** Identity, immigration and left-behind places drove Leave `[hobolt2016]`, `[goodwinheath2016]`, `[becker2017]`. **Contested.** Trade shocks `[colantone2018brexit]` versus austerity `[fetzer2019]` versus identity `[carl2019]`. **Gaps.** Contagion effects `[walter2021brexit]` and new Brexit identities `[hobolt2020]`, `[sobolewskaford2020]`.

## 17. EU foreign policy and normative power
**Claimed.** A civilian `[duchene1972]` and normative power `[manners2002]`. **Contested.** Realist `[hydeprice2006]` and market-power `[damro2012]` critiques; the Brussels effect `[bradford2020]`. **Gaps.** Geopoliticization and industrial policy `[meunier2019]`, `[mcnamara2023]`; internal spoilers `[orenstein2017]`.

## 18. Regulatory state and governance
**Claimed.** The EU as a regulatory state `[majone1994]`, `[majone1996]` caught in joint-decision traps `[scharpf1988]`, `[scharpf2006]`. **Settled.** Agencification and networks `[levifaur2011]`, `[wonkarittberger2010]`. **Contested.** Interest group influence `[dur2008]`, `[durbernhagen2015]`. **Gaps.** Governance in the shadow of hierarchy `[borzel2010jcms]`.

## 19. Differentiated integration
**Settled.** Differentiation is a lasting feature `[stubb1996]`, `[leuffen2013]`, `[schimmelfennig2015di]`. **Contested.** Instrumental versus constitutional logics `[schimmelfennigwinzen2014]`; limits for the rule of law `[kelemen2019]`. **Gaps.** Brexit as differentiated disintegration `[schimmelfennig2018brexit]`.
